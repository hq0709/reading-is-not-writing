## 1_introduction paragraph 2 — ACCEPTED

**before**

Linear probes are the standard instrument for asking what a vision-language model (VLM) represents: fit a classifier on the activations of a chosen layer and read a concept out \citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2022probing}. In medical imaging the probe often succeeds---clinical findings such as pleural effusion or cardiomegaly are linearly decodable from the visual representation of general-purpose and medical VLMs \citep{zhu2026lost,pedersen2026probes}. Activation steering promises the converse operation: write the probe direction back into the representation and move the model's behaviour \citep{turner2023steering,panickssery2024caa}. Together the two operations would make a probe direction a handle on the concept: read it to detect, write it to control. Whether that handle exists is the question of this paper.

The question is not answered by showing that steering ``works''. A write can change an answer for reasons unrelated to the concept's label: any perturbation of matched size may push a yes/no answer, and a direction fitted for one finding may carry a generic abnormality signal shared with other findings \citep{pahde2025navigating}. Across 21 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in 56\% and answerable in 61\% of cells but owned in 12\%; the same procedure owns 88\% of natural-object cells; the direction the model answers along is a handle and is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction on chest radiographs; and checkpoints that share a vision tower, and therefore a write vector, still differ in what they own. Ownership is decided by the reader of the representation. The evaluation that establishes this is released as a benchmark for concept writability in medical VLMs.

**after**

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make the same direction a handle on the concept when written back. Across 21 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in 56\% and answerable in 61\% of cells but owned in 12\%. The same procedure owns 88\% of natural-object cells. The direction the model answers along is a handle. On chest radiographs, it is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction. Checkpoints that share a vision tower, and therefore a write vector, still differ in what they own. The reader of the representation decides ownership. We release the evaluation that establishes these findings as a benchmark for concept writability in medical VLMs.
