\caption{\textbf{Small and fine-grained objects, and the difficulty-matched comparison.} The six categories---handbag, book, backpack, traffic light, knife, and cell phone---were fixed by a rule on the label counts and the mean instance areas of the fixed cohorts alone, registered before any of them was scored, as were the two matching windows; Appendix~\ref{app:repro-protocol} states the rule in full. They are fitted, lifted, written and graded exactly like the six easy objects, in the same blocks and rows, and their clean-answer AUROC is measured on the same calibration rows and by the same rule as every other cell. The middle panel separates the easy objects of every COCO block, which is the grid-wide number, from the easy objects of the 6 blocks the fine-grained cells come from, which is the pool the fine-grained rate is compared with. The bottom panel pairs each chest cell with every natural-image cell whose matching variables lie within 0.05 of it and reports two estimators of the ownership difference, each with its own interval. The \emph{pooled} difference is the ownership rate among the matched chest cells minus the rate among the natural-image cells that served as a partner at least once: it is the difference of the two rates printed to its left. The \emph{matched} difference is the mean over matched chest cells of that cell's own ownership minus the ownership rate of its own partner set, so a chest cell with many partners and one with few count equally. The two answer different questions and neither can be obtained from the other. \emph{Chest cells} and \emph{partners} give the effective sample of both: matched chest cells of all chest cells, and distinct natural-image cells used as a partner of all of them. Both intervals are percentile intervals over 5{,}000 draws of a cluster bootstrap whose unit is the model. Probe selectivity is the probe AUROC minus the mean of the twenty random-label control AUROCs on the calibration rows. The last panel stratifies the same cells on clean-answer AUROC instead of matching on it, at the bin edges 0.50, 0.70, 0.80, 0.90, which are fixed in the released analysis code and are the conventional reading points of an AUROC. The last bin is closed on the right, every cell of each pool falls in exactly one bin, and none is dropped: that is what separates the stratification from the matching above it. Its interval is the same cluster bootstrap over models.}
