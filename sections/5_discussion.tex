\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Under original yes/no prompts, Qwen Effusion is a reproducible, non-generic writer, but Nodule is stronger. The six gates support neither a named owner nor a shared alias beyond random controls; Consolidation shows no reliable input-displacement closure. Answer movement and selective concept writing require different evidence.

Response diagnostics connect attribution to answer behavior (Appendix~\ref{app:response-diagnostics}). All six ownership margins remain negative in logit coordinates, every matched-direction answer-score AUROC point estimate falls below baseline, and Effusion raises the answer margin in both label groups. The leading singular component contains 90.51\% of logit-effect energy. In the crossover, random and sham directions share aggregate mapping-odd dominance (Section~\ref{sec:encoding-results}; Appendix~\ref{app:encoding}). Concentration and odd dominance alone identify neither a shared causal channel nor clinical specificity.

Under both \emph{show} A/B prompts, Mass exceeds all five clinical competitors with positive simultaneous lower bounds. Larger effects among 119 random directions defeat the registered cross-wording and original-wording specificity conjunctions. Both \emph{is} coded margins are negative. Explicit \emph{show} yes/no has a similar observed margin, with paired A/B contrasts spanning zero. Under \emph{is}, A/B instructions improve clinical margins relative to explicit yes/no, which improves them relative to the original anchor. Wording and response instructions shape competition; A/B symbols alone do not explain the advantage. Equation~\ref{eq:margin} describes attribution under a specified task interface.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The discovery screen reused 200 ownership-cohort patients across six questions; its pointwise intervals and 20 random controls remain descriptive. The crossover's mapping-energy endpoint uses four calibration-eligible questions; Effusion and Cardiomegaly remain descriptive. Mass confirmation uses independent patients and 119 random controls, but its paired prompt contrasts are descriptive and intervals spanning zero do not establish equivalence. All seven prompts meet the registered calibration criterion on 200 patients with 15 positives. Confirmation has 12 positives among 200 patients, limiting discrimination precision: every paired AUROC-change interval spans zero, while Brier error increases in every cell.

The evidence covers ChestX-ray14, two 7B architectures, one final visual locus, linear readouts, and relative-token interventions; Mass confirmation fixes one model, finding, and dose. Report-derived labels, acquisition correlations, and mean pooling limit clinical and spatial interpretation. Bootstrap uncertainty conditions on one fitted direction bundle. Consolidation's small displacement, $|\alpha|$ of order $10^{-4}$ with a lower bound crossing zero, limits closure resolution. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} records reproducibility details.
