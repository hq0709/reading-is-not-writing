\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Under original yes/no prompts, Qwen Effusion is a reproducible, non-generic writer, but Nodule is stronger. The six gates support neither a named owner nor a shared alias beyond random controls; the 50-pair Consolidation study finds no reliable input-displacement closure.

The new 100-pair cohort asks whether natural Consolidation-positive and negative inputs supply a behavioral difference for component closure to explain. Positive paired opportunity remains unresolved in both models on the same 200 images (Table~\ref{tab:paired-opportunity}). Natural paired behavior should therefore be measured before interpreting component closure in a target population. This boundary concerns the new cohort; the earlier 50-pair closure result retains its original scope.

Negative logit ownership margins and concentrated response energy persist (Appendix~\ref{app:response-diagnostics}); random and sham directions share mapping-odd dominance (Appendix~\ref{app:encoding}). These patterns leave the causal channel unresolved.

Mass exceeds all five clinical competitors under both \emph{show} A/B prompts with positive simultaneous lower bounds. Larger effects among 119 random directions defeat both registered specificity criteria. Both \emph{is} coded margins are negative. Explicit \emph{show} yes/no has a similar observed margin; paired A/B contrasts span zero. Under \emph{is}, A/B instructions improve clinical margins relative to explicit yes/no, which improves them relative to the original anchor. Wording and response instructions shape competition.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Paired opportunity uses metadata-matched longitudinal studies, retaining unrecorded differences. The intervals leave positive opportunity and the concordance difference unresolved. The Qwen-informed LLaVA follow-up compares native model configurations, including their training and preprocessing, on the same patients; its evidence concerns these complete configurations in a shared cohort. Concordance gives half credit to ties under the finite-precision evaluation configured for BF16 (Appendix~\ref{app:paired-opportunity}).

The discovery screen reused 200 patients and 20 random controls; crossover energy covers four calibration-eligible questions. Mass confirmation has 12 positives among 200 independent patients: every AUROC-change interval spans zero and Brier error increases in every cell. Its paired prompt contrasts remain descriptive, and intervals spanning zero do not establish equivalence. Evidence covers ChestX-ray14, two 7B models, one visual locus, and linear readouts; Mass confirmation fixes one model, finding, and dose. Report-derived labels, acquisition correlations, and mean pooling limit clinical interpretation. Bootstrap uncertainty conditions on one direction bundle. Small Consolidation displacement limits closure resolution (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).
