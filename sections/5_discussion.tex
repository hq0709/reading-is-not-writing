\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

Clinical attribution is conditional on direction, question, and prompt. Under the original yes/no prompts, Qwen Effusion is a reproducible, non-generic writer whose clinical competitor Nodule is stronger. Across the six gates under those prompts, the matched response surface supports neither a named owner nor a shared alias beyond random controls, and the Consolidation gate finds no reliable input-displacement closure. Semantic competition makes these conclusions precise by comparing equally constructed directions at a common locus, dose, patient set, and endpoint.

The response diagnostics connect this attribution problem to answer behavior (Appendix~\ref{app:response-diagnostics}). All six ownership margins remain negative in logit coordinates, while every matched-direction answer-score AUROC point estimate falls below baseline. Effusion raises the answer margin in both label groups. The leading singular component contains 90.51\% of the logit-effect matrix energy. These descriptive patterns motivate testing how the response depends on the answer interface; matrix concentration alone does not identify a shared causal channel.

The crossover reveals two distinct properties of that interface (Section~\ref{sec:encoding-results}; Appendix~\ref{app:encoding}). Across four calibration-eligible questions, aggregate response energy favors the component that reverses raw A-minus-B sign when the finding-to-letter mapping is exchanged. Random and sham perturbations share this odd dominance, so the decomposition establishes neither clinical specificity nor encoding invariance. Separately, Mass's clinical logit-effect margin changes from $-0.7181$ under standard yes/no to $+0.2825$ with A-present and $+0.2900$ with B-present. The observed clinical ranking therefore depends on the prompt package. Equation~\ref{eq:margin} describes a competition under a specified task interface; a vector's read label alone cannot supply that attribution.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Mass was identified by screening six questions on 200 intervention patients reused from the ownership cohort. Its pointwise intervals and 20 random controls have descriptive status, distinct from the original simultaneous ownership test. Standard and coded prompts change wording and response instructions as well as answer symbols, so the contrast does not isolate answer encoding causally. Independent, prospectively locked confirmation remains pending. The mapping-energy endpoint uses four questions eligible on 200 patient-disjoint calibration patients; Effusion and Cardiomegaly remain descriptive.

The broader evidence covers ChestX-ray14, two 7B architectures, one final visual locus, linear readouts, relative-token interventions, and the original yes/no plus Qwen coded prompts. Report-derived labels and acquisition correlations limit clinical interpretation; mean pooling lacks spatial localization, and answer shifts measure a behavioral pathway rather than diagnostic quality. Patient bootstrap uncertainty conditions on one fitted direction bundle. The Consolidation displacement is small, with $|\alpha|$ of order $10^{-4}$ and a lower bound crossing zero, limiting closure resolution. Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} records the reproducibility details. The next scientific test is whether Mass retains its prompt-conditioned advantage on independent patients under a locked control family, establishing the basis for a natural-displacement comparison.
