\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{A reproducible writer can remain semantically non-specific.}
Qwen's Effusion normal produces a large response, beats matched random and sham controls, and reproduces at a locked dose on ChestX-ray14 test patients absent from the original intervention cohort. Because the maximum over fixed alternative clinical normals is reliably stronger, the response cannot be attributed specifically to Effusion under our criterion. A vector trained to read a label can therefore be a genuine writer without being the uniquely named writer.

\paragraph{Semantic competitors distinguish efficacy from attribution.}
Matched clinical directions are load-bearing whenever a probe normal carries a semantic name. Random vectors test the ambient geometry, and a coordinate permutation tests one construction artifact, but neither is a plausible direction learned from another clinical label with the same pipeline. The resulting hierarchy is constructive: non-semantic controls support a non-generic effect under the registered comparison, while semantic competitors test attribution. Comparisons should share the locus, dose, rows, endpoint, and a familywise statistic over the fixed alternative set.

\paragraph{The causal object is a response-surface contrast.}
Probe-vector studies often report one labeled direction and one downstream response, making semantic identity appear intrinsic to the vector. Equation~\ref{eq:margin} instead treats a direction's name as a hypothesis about its position in a matched direction--question response surface. An off-diagonal effect can be large without invalidating the intervention; it changes what the intervention identifies. Diagonal advantage supports semantic ownership, whereas a strong shared row or column points to an answer-sensitive causal alias. This reframing preserves useful steering effects while demanding the comparison needed to name them.

\paragraph{What semantic competition may reveal.}
Failure of specificity is compatible with several mechanisms: correlated findings, shared answer-sensitive subspaces, or a readout normal that exploits discriminative variance broader than the concept it names. The present experiment identifies the mismatch but does not choose among these mechanisms. Its value is to turn that ambiguity into a measurable object: competition among clinical normals can reveal whether an apparent causal direction is privileged, shared, or dominated. The reported supplement estimates one response-surface row; a full read-direction by write-question matrix would distinguish question-specific ownership from a shared causal alias.

\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
The evidence covers ChestX-ray14, two 7B architectures, one consumed final visual locus, Effusion in both models, Edema in LLaVA, fixed yes/no prompts, a linear readout, and relative-token interventions. ChestX-ray14 labels are mined from reports; patient-disjoint splits prevent identity leakage but not label noise or acquisition correlations. Mean pooling does not localize findings spatially, and mean $P(yes)$ measures one answer pathway rather than diagnostic accuracy or report quality. The specificity supplement locks one Qwen dose and five clinical alternatives; a broader vocabulary or other doses are new endpoints. Its patient bootstrap conditions on one fitted direction bundle rather than propagating direction-estimation uncertainty. The familywise result rejects Effusion specificity within this fixed family, but does not identify Nodule as the semantic owner of the shared response.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and ethics.}
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} records prompts, row-selection hashes, seeds, doses, model revisions, run identifiers, and GPU wall time. Immutable snapshots and terminal receipts bind each result to its environment and inputs. This work evaluates interpretability evidence, not clinical diagnostic performance. Its safety implication is methodological: a decodable feature should be treated as clinically operative only after intervention controls establish direction specificity.
