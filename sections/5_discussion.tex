\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Reading is not writing.}
The campaign turns the seed observation into a property of current medical vision-language models. At the block the language model consumes, clinical concepts are linearly readable in most checkpoints and the models answer the corresponding questions above chance, yet writing the read direction back rarely moves the concept's own answer more than a competing clinical direction does. The three graded outcomes separate cleanly: readability is decided by the visual representation, answerability by the whole model, and ownership by how the language model reads a perturbation of that representation. The same-write experiment isolates the last of these: Gemma~3 and MedGemma checkpoints that share a vision tower receive byte-identical write vectors and differ only in the reader, and ownership differs with the reader.

\paragraph{The deficit is clinical.}
The natural-object control rules out the mechanism as the cause. With identical cohort construction, direction fitting, gates, dose, and decision rules, COCO objects are owned in 87\% of cells with monotone dose response and negligible sham and random effects. Chest-radiograph concepts are read by the same probes with comparable selectivity, but their write effects are shared across concepts: in most cells a competing clinical direction moves the answer more, and the concept write behaves like a generic ``more abnormal'' signal rather than a labelled one. Report-derived concept labels, disease co-occurrence, and acquisition correlations are plausible sources of this sharing; the evaluation does not identify which, and it does not need to in order to grade the outcome.

\paragraph{Ownership is a reader property.}
Where ownership does occur it is checkpoint-specific and concept-specific: Qwen2.5-VL-72B on NIH, Lingshu on CheXpert, and single concepts in a handful of other checkpoints. It does not transfer from NIH to CheXpert for the same checkpoint and direction construction, and different checkpoints own different concepts. Effusion, the seed concept and the most readable one, is owned in five of 36 chest blocks and anti-owned by every Qwen2.5-VL size. The leaderboard therefore ranks readers, not representations, and a checkpoint that owns a clinical concept is one whose language model reads that specific direction rather than a shared abnormality direction.

\paragraph{The evaluation as a benchmark.}
Every number in the campaign was produced by one registered procedure with logged acceptance decisions; the adapters differ only in naming the consumed block and its token layout. The preflight gates caught the measurement facts that would otherwise have distorted the comparison---bf16 answer logits on a coarse lattice, shape-dependent kernels, and templates that a checkpoint cannot map without an image---and turned them into declared dispositions rather than silent errors. The leaderboard of readable, answerable, and owned concepts, with its controls, is what we propose as the concept-writability benchmark for medical vision-language models. Passing it means that a clinical concept the model can read is one the model actually uses when the concept's own direction is written.

\paragraph{Scope.}
The findings condition on the fixed direction construction (a 512-d projection and logistic normals with three fit seeds), one relative dose per module, six yes/no and A/B templates, and pooled consumed-token features at one primary and one connector locus; the two largest checkpoints are graded on NIH (72B) or deferred (90B). Bootstrap intervals quantify uncertainty over patients, not over retraining of directions. Selectivity against type-conditioned random-label controls measures an advantage over nuisance-label tasks, not freedom from confounding by co-occurring disease. The measured ownership establishes whether a labelled direction is the model's own; it is not a claim about diagnostic benefit, which the seed study showed to be absent for Mass steering.
