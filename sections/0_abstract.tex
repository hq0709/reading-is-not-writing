A linear probe reads a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation. Writing the probe direction back tests whether it is a handle on the concept: the write must move the concept's answer more than writes along every other direction constructed the same way. On chest radiographs, the probe direction almost never passes this test. We define three criteria at the visual block the language model consumes: whether a concept is \emph{readable} (probe AUROC above matched random-label controls), \emph{answerable} (the model's own yes/no answer beats chance), and \emph{owned} (a fixed-dose write along the probe direction moves the concept's answer more than the 95th percentile of 119 random directions, a coordinate-permutation sham, and every competing clinical direction, with simultaneous max-$T$ bounds). Across \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints from nine model families (3B to 72B) on NIH ChestX-ray14 and CheXpert Plus, clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% of checkpoint--concept cells, answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\%, and owned in only \cfChestOwnedPct\%. Two measurements characterise this gap. At the fixed dose, the probe direction clears the random and sham references in \cfChestRefMet{} of the \cfChestAnswerableN{} chest cells. Of those \cfChestRefMet{} cells, \cfChestOwned{} are owned, \cfChestRefStrong{} lose to a competing clinical direction, and \cfChestRefUnres{} are unresolved. A competing clinical direction moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does in \cfChestCompetitor{} cells. Of these \cfChestCompetitor{} verdicts, \cfChestBelowStrong{} occur where the concept's own write fails to clear the references. The seed result reproduces on new patients (ownership $\cfSeedEffO$, 95\% CI $[\cfSeedEffOLo,\cfSeedEffOHi]$). On COCO objects, the same procedure yields ownership in \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of cells. There, \cfOwnShareCoco\% of the positive answer change a write produces lands on its own question, against \cfOwnShareNih\% on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert. The write mechanism is intact. Six small or fine-grained object categories are owned at the rate of the easy ones (\cfFgFineOwned{} of \cfFgFineCells{} against \cfFgEasyOwned{} of \cfFgEasyCells{}). Matching chest cells to natural-image cells with the same probe selectivity pairs all \cfFgMatchSelN{} chest cells and leaves ownership rates at \cfFgMatchSelChest{} against \cfFgMatchSelNat{}. Also matching on the model's own clean-answer accuracy leaves \cfFgMatchBothChest{} against \cfFgMatchBothNat{}. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts and not to task difficulty. A direction fitted to the model's own clean answer margin provides a handle in the same representation. It is owned where the probe direction is not. On the chest sets, it reads the clinical label less accurately than the probe (median AUROC $\cfValAurocAnsChest$ against $\cfValAurocProbeChest$). It reads the model's own answer more accurately ($\cfValAurocAnsCleanChest$ against $\cfValAurocProbeCleanChest$). On chest radiographs, this answer direction is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction in raw model space (cosine $\cfAnsCosChestMed$ against $\cfAnsCosCocoMed$ on natural images, median over concepts within each block, then over blocks). Under the training-feature covariance, it remains far from the probe direction (whitened cosine $\cfValCosSigmaChestMin$--$\cfValCosSigmaChestMax$ against $\cfValCosSigmaCoco$, median over cells). Gemma~3 and MedGemma checkpoints of different sizes share one vision tower and therefore have identical probes and write vectors. Yet they own different concepts. Crossing the two factors---pairing one checkpoint's vision tower with the other's connector and language model, verified tensor by tensor---leaves every clinical concept unwritable in all four combinations of tower and reader (\cfSwapChestOwned{} of \cfSwapChestCells{} chest cells). Natural-image concepts stay writable in three of them (\cfSwapCocoOwned{} of \cfSwapCocoCells{} cells). On NIH, the mean absolute reader effect is \cfSwapReaderNihMin{}--\cfSwapReaderNihMax{} against a tower effect of \cfSwapTowerNihMin{}. Ownership is a property of the representation and the reader together, not of either alone. The protocol, acceptance gates, and per-checkpoint leaderboard form a reusable benchmark for concept writability in medical vision-language models.
