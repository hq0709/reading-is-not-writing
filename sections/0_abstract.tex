A readable clinical direction need not selectively write its named concept into a model's answer. We formulate this reader--writer problem as semantic competition among matched clinical directions, conditional on question and prompt. At the consumed final visual block of LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, three ChestX-ray14 cells have AUROC 0.7742--0.8009 and positive controlled selectivity. Under the original yes/no prompts, neither LLaVA cell clears random/sham controls; Qwen Effusion does so twice, yet Nodule moves the Effusion answer more. Under those prompts, the six-gate sequence yields no owned concept, shared alias beyond random controls, or reliable input-displacement closure. A Qwen answer-encoding crossover then tests task-interface sensitivity. Across four calibration-eligible questions, mapping-even minus mapping-odd response energy is $-2.1786$ (95\% CI $[-2.4061,-1.9810]$). Random and sham directions are also odd-dominant, so this aggregate establishes neither clinical specificity nor encoding invariance. In a descriptive six-question screen on 200 reused intervention patients, Mass's clinical logit-effect margin changes from $-0.7181$ under standard yes/no to $+0.2825$ with A-present and $+0.2900$ with B-present. Wording and response instructions co-vary across these prompt packages; independent confirmation is pending. Clinical attribution therefore requires testing direction--question--prompt combinations, with matched semantic competitors and mechanism evidence, rather than assigning causal meaning from a vector's read label.
