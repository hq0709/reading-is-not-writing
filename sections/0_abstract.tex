In a chest-radiograph vision--language model, a direction trained to read Effusion writes a reproducible Effusion answer---yet a direction trained to read Nodule writes the same answer more strongly. Probe-vector steering often treats the readable direction as the privileged writer for the same concept. We test this reader--writer assumption at the exact final visual block consumed downstream. The Qwen--Effusion cell provides the strongest non-generic intervention result: it is robustly decodable, exceeds matched random and coordinate-permutation controls, and reproduces at its locked dose on a patient-disjoint, within-dataset cohort of 400 patients, changing mean $P(yes)$ by 0.1945. Semantic competition nevertheless defeats Effusion-specific attribution. Nodule is the point-estimate strongest competitor at 0.2519, and the Effusion ownership contrast $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}$ is $-0.0573$ (95\% patient-bootstrap CI $[-0.0694,-0.0450]$). Two LLaVA cells expose an earlier mismatch: both are robust readers, but neither satisfies the random-and-sham conjunction. Across the three model--concept cells, AUROC ranges from 0.7742 to 0.8009 and every controlled-selectivity interval excludes zero. Thus a probe normal can be a reproducible, non-generic write direction without being specific to the concept it reads. Causal interpretation of probe-vector steering requires clinically meaningful alternatives, not only non-semantic controls.
