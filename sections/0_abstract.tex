A Qwen crossover reveals prompt-dependent clinical competition. Confirmation on 200 independent patients reproduces Mass's clinical advantage under both \emph{show} A/B prompts, with positive simultaneous lower bounds, but larger effects among 119 random directions defeat both registered specificity criteria. Replacing \emph{show} with \emph{is} reverses the clinical advantage; explicit \emph{show} yes/no has a similar observed margin. Wording and response instructions shape clinical competition. Across confirmation prompts, AUROC-change intervals cross zero and Brier error increases. Clinical attribution requires direction--question--prompt comparisons with competitors, behavioral opportunity, and mechanism evidence; steering efficacy and diagnostic benefit remain separate questions.
