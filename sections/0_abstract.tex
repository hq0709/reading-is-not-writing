A linear probe reads a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation. Writing the probe direction back into the same representation tests whether it is a handle on the concept: it must move the concept's own answer more than every other direction constructed the same way. On chest radiographs, the probe direction almost never passes this test. We define three criteria at the visual block the language model consumes: whether a concept is \emph{readable} (probe AUROC above matched random-label controls), \emph{answerable} (the model's own yes/no answer beats chance), and \emph{owned} (a fixed-dose write along the probe direction moves the concept's answer more than the 95th percentile of 119 random directions, a coordinate-permutation sham, and every competing clinical direction, with simultaneous max-$T$ bounds). Across \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints from nine model families (3B to 72B) on NIH ChestX-ray14 and CheXpert Plus, clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% of checkpoint--concept cells, answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\%, and owned in only \cfChestOwnedPct\%. In two thirds of the cells, a competing clinical direction moves the concept's answer more than the concept's own direction does. The seed result reproduces on new patients (ownership $\cfSeedEffO$, 95\% CI $[\cfSeedEffOLo,\cfSeedEffOHi]$). On COCO objects, the same procedure owns \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of cells. Three quarters of every write land on its own question. The write mechanism is intact. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts. A direction fitted to the model's own answers provides a handle in the same representation. It is owned where the probe direction is not. On chest radiographs, this answer direction is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction (median block cosine $\cfAnsCosChestMed$ against $\cfAnsCosCocoMed$ on natural images). Gemma~3 and MedGemma checkpoints of different sizes share one vision tower and therefore have identical probes and write vectors. Yet they own different concepts. The reader of the representation---the connector and language model---decides what a written direction does; the representation does not. The protocol, acceptance gates, and per-checkpoint leaderboard form a reusable benchmark for concept writability in medical vision-language models.
