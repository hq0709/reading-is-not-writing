A readable clinical direction need not selectively write its concept into a model's answer. We formulate this reader--writer problem as semantic competition among matched clinical directions, conditional on question and prompt. At the consumed final visual block of LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B, three ChestX-ray14 cells have AUROC 0.7742--0.8009 and positive controlled selectivity. Under original yes/no prompts, neither LLaVA cell clears random/sham controls; Qwen Effusion does so twice, yet Nodule moves the Effusion answer more. The six gates yield no owned concept, shared alias beyond random controls, or reliable input-displacement closure. A Qwen crossover shows mapping-odd dominance shared by random and sham directions and a prompt-dependent Mass clinical ranking. Confirmation on 200 independent patients reproduces Mass's clinical advantage under both \emph{show} A/B prompts, with positive simultaneous lower bounds, but larger effects among 119 random directions defeat both registered specificity criteria. Replacing \emph{show} with \emph{is} reverses the clinical advantage; explicit \emph{show} yes/no has a similar observed margin. Wording and response instructions shape clinical competition. Across confirmation prompts, AUROC-change intervals cross zero and Brier error increases. Clinical attribution requires direction--question--prompt comparisons with matched competitors and mechanism evidence; steering efficacy and diagnostic benefit are separate questions.
