\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept out of a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle: across 21 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in 56\% and answerable in 61\% of cells but owned in 12\%, while the same procedure owns 88\% of natural-object cells; the direction the model answers along is a handle and is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction on chest radiographs; and checkpoints that share a vision tower still differ in what they own, so ownership is decided by the reader of the representation. The evaluation is released as a benchmark for concept writability in medical VLMs.
