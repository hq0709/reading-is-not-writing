\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle. Across \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% and answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\% of cells but owned in \cfChestOwnedPct\%. The same procedure owns \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of natural-object cells. The direction the model answers along is a handle and is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction in raw model space on chest radiographs. Checkpoints sharing a vision tower still differ in what they own, so the reader of the representation decides ownership.
