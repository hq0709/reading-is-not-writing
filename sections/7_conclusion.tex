\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle. Across 21 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in 56\% and answerable in 61\% of cells but owned in 12\%. The same procedure owns 88\% of natural-object cells. The direction the model answers along is a handle and is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction on chest radiographs. Checkpoints sharing a vision tower still differ in what they own, so the reader of the representation decides ownership.
