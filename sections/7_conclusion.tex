\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept out of a vision-language model's visual representation does not mean the model can be made to use it by writing the same direction back. Across 21 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in 55\% and answerable in 60\% of cells but owned in 12\%; the same procedure owns 87\% of natural-object cells; and checkpoints that share a vision tower, and therefore a write vector, still differ in what they own. Ownership is decided by the reader of the representation. The evaluation that establishes this---matched clinical competitors, a random--sham reference family, simultaneous bounds, and registered acceptance gates at the consumed visual block---is released as a benchmark for concept writability in medical VLMs.
