\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept from a vision-language model's visual representation does not make its probe direction a handle on that concept. Across \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable in \cfChestReadablePct\% and answerable in \cfChestAnswerablePct\% of cells but owned in \cfChestOwnedPct\%. A direction fitted for another finding wins the comparison the concept's own direction is built to win. The same procedure owns \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of natural-object cells, and the gap survives matching the two pools on the model's own answer ability. Checkpoints that share a vision tower, and therefore write identical vectors, own different concepts. The model's own answer direction is a handle where the label direction is not: on chest radiographs it is nearly orthogonal to the probe direction in raw model space (median cosine $\cfAnsCosChestMed$, against $\cfAnsCosCocoMed$ on natural images). Readability is a property of a classifier fitted to the representation, not a handle on the concept that classifier names.
