\section{Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

\paragraph{For steering and clinical evaluation.}
A probe direction that reads a clinical concept is not a handle on it. Probe accuracy and answer accuracy, the two quantities most often reported for medical VLMs, do not establish whether a handle exists: both are high in this grid, yet ownership is 12\%. A steering study that reports a large answer change against random and sham controls shows that the write reaches the answer. It does not show that the label names the direction: a direction fitted for another finding at the same locus, dose, and patients moves the same answer as much or more in two thirds of clinical cells. The matched-competitor comparison is the missing test and is cheap once the competitors' directions exist. Studies that use probe normals as interventions should report the write matrix $W$. Ownership $O_q$ should be the quantity compared across layers, doses, and models. A natural-image control should accompany every clinical write so that a failed handle is not mistaken for a failed mechanism. A checkpoint whose Effusion direction moves the Effusion answer less than its Nodule direction does has no Effusion handle at this locus. Its Effusion answer moves with every finding's direction. The write matrix shows by how much. The direction it does respond to is the one fitted to its own answers, at cosine $0.1$ to the probe direction. An evaluation that certifies concept use for deployment must include this test.

\paragraph{For model builders.}
The same write vector produces different ownership in different language models. A vision tower that exposes a finding linearly does not make its direction a handle. The connector and language model decide what the written direction does. Ownership on the chest sets rises with the reader in some families (Qwen2.5-VL from 7B to 72B, Lingshu from 7B to 32B) and not in others (Gemma~3, InternVL3.5). Ownership appears most often with medical fine-tuning of the reader (Lingshu, MedGemma on CheXpert). The benchmark grades a new checkpoint in about ten GPU-hours per chest dataset. It grades the reader: ownership on the chest sets rises with reader scale in Qwen2.5-VL from 7B to 72B and Lingshu from 7B to 32B. Gemma~3 and InternVL3.5 show no such rise. Ownership appears most often with medical fine-tuning of the reader (Lingshu, MedGemma on CheXpert). Evaluating a new checkpoint takes about ten GPU-hours per chest dataset.
