\section{Implications}
\label{sec:implications}

\paragraph{For steering and clinical evaluation.}
A probe direction that reads a clinical concept is not a handle on it, and the two quantities most often reported for medical VLMs, probe accuracy and answer accuracy, do not say whether one exists: both are high in this grid and ownership is 12\%. A steering study that reports a large answer change against random and sham controls has shown that the write reaches the answer; it has not shown that the label names the direction, because a direction fitted for another finding at the same locus, dose, and patients moves the same answer as much or more in two thirds of clinical cells. The matched-competitor comparison is the missing test and it is cheap once the competitors' directions exist: the write matrix $W$ should be reported whenever probe normals are used as interventions, ownership $O_q$ should be the quantity compared across layers, doses, and models, and a natural-image control should accompany every clinical write so that a failed handle is not mistaken for a failed mechanism. A checkpoint whose Effusion direction moves the Effusion answer less than its Nodule direction does has no Effusion handle at this locus; its Effusion answer moves with every finding's direction, and the write matrix shows by how much. An evaluation that certifies concept use for deployment has to include this test.

\paragraph{For model builders.}
The same write vector produces different ownership in different language models. A vision tower that exposes a finding linearly does not make its direction a handle; the connector and the language model decide what the written direction does. Ownership on the chest sets rises with the reader in some families (Qwen2.5-VL from 7B to 72B, Lingshu from 7B to 32B) and not in others (Gemma~3, InternVL3.5), and medical fine-tuning of the reader (Lingshu, MedGemma on CheXpert) is where ownership appears most often. The benchmark grades the reader; it can be run on a new checkpoint in about ten GPU-hours per chest dataset.
