\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Semantic competition tests whether a readable direction is privileged for its named response. The original six gates establish a reader--writer mismatch under fixed yes/no prompts, including a reproducible Qwen writer without matched ownership. The answer-encoding crossover adds task-interface sensitivity: aggregate odd dominance is shared by random and sham controls, while Mass's clinical advantage appears under both coded prompts in a descriptive screen awaiting independent confirmation. Clinical attribution belongs to a direction--question--prompt combination and requires matched competitors and mechanism evidence. A vector's read label alone is insufficient to name its causal role.
