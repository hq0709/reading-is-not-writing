\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A probe normal can read a clinical concept, write a reproducible behavioral change, and still fail to identify that change with its label. At the consumed final visual block, all three registered cells are robust readers. Qwen Effusion further beats matched random and sham controls in two cohorts, yet semantic competition shows that the maximum over five fixed clinical alternatives is reliably stronger; Nodule realizes the point-estimate maximum. The result separates intervention efficacy from semantic attribution and shifts the causal object from a named vector to its contrast within a matched direction--question response surface. The full matched surface is the decisive next test: diagonal advantage would support semantic ownership, whereas a shared row or column would expose answer-sensitive aliasing. For probe-vector steering studies like these, clinically meaningful alternatives tested at the same representation, dose, rows, and endpoint are what turn a named perturbation into a direction-specific causal claim.
