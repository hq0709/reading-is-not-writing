\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Semantic competition tests whether a readable direction is privileged for its named response. The original six gates establish a reader--writer mismatch under fixed yes/no prompts, including a reproducible Qwen writer without matched ownership. Mass confirmation reproduces clinical advantage under \emph{show} A/B prompts, yet larger random effects defeat registered specificity and \emph{is} wording reverses the clinical ranking. Wording and response instructions shape competition. Clinical attribution belongs to a direction--question--prompt combination and requires competitors and mechanism evidence; steering efficacy alone supplies neither that attribution nor diagnostic benefit.
