\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Reading a clinical concept out of a vision-language model's visual representation does not mean the model can be made to use it by writing the same direction back. Across 22 checkpoints and two chest-radiograph datasets, concepts are readable and answerable in most cells and owned in 13\%; the same procedure owns 87\% of natural-object cells, and checkpoints that share a vision tower and therefore a write vector still differ in what they own. The evaluation that establishes this---fixed loci, matched direction construction, seven acceptance gates, a random and sham reference family, and simultaneous ownership bounds---is released as a benchmark, so that concept writability can be graded for any new model with one adapter.
