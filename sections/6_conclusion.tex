\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Semantic competition tests whether a readable direction is privileged for its named response. Six gates establish a reader--writer mismatch, including a reproducible Qwen writer without matched ownership. Fresh paired inputs leave positive opportunity unresolved in both models, motivating behavioral measurement before component-closure interpretation. Mass's clinical advantage under \emph{show} A/B prompts reverses under \emph{is}, while larger random effects defeat specificity. Wording and response instructions shape competition. Clinical attribution requires direction--question--prompt comparisons, competitors, and mechanism evidence; steering efficacy and diagnostic benefit remain separate questions.
