\section{Campaign details}
\label{app:campaign}

This appendix lists the checkpoints and their consumed loci, the acceptance decisions, the coverage of the campaign, and the per-concept results of every completed block.

\subsection{Labels, registration, and artifact}
\label{app:cf-labels}

\paragraph{Labels.} Concept labels are the report-derived labels shipped with each dataset (NIH ChestX-ray14 finding labels; the CheXpert labeler output distributed with CheXpert Plus; COCO instance annotations). A row is positive when the label is 1, negative when it is 0, and \emph{unknown} when the labeler returns uncertain or blank; unknown rows are excluded from probe fitting, selectivity, and answer AUROC, and are written and scored like every other row. Cohorts are fixed manifests drawn with recorded seeds, patient-disjoint between training and test and disjoint from every seed-study cohort; per-concept counts are in Table~\ref{tab:cf-prevalence}.

\paragraph{Registration.} The protocol was frozen before any block of the grid was scored: the loci, the projection and probe settings, the dose, the reference family, the six templates, the module set, the seven gates, and the three decision rules are those of the registered protocol file and its code commit, and the seed study's crossover and confirmation analyses were registered before they were run (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). Everything reported per block is produced by one analysis pass from the packaged outcomes; the leaderboard, the write-flow share, and the per-image examples are descriptive summaries of those outputs added after the grid was complete.

\paragraph{Artifact.} The release contains the evaluation code and adapters, the registered protocol file, the cohort manifests with their seeds, the fitted directions, the per-image outcomes of every module (write, dose, refit, locus, and template rows), every block's scorecard and coverage file, and the rendered prompts; a new checkpoint is added with one adapter that names its consumed block and passes the gates.

\subsection{Acceptance decisions}
\label{app:cf-gates}

All \cfWriteBlocks{} completed blocks pass checks (A), (B), (C), and (G) with zero determinism error, zero change at $\alpha=0$, and fp32-versus-model logit differences below 0.13. Check (D) exceeds the 0.25-logit tolerance for the Qwen3-VL, Gemma~3, MedGemma, InternVL, and Llama families (up to 1.84 logits for Gemma~3 12B); these are recorded as declared deviations and neutralised by the fixed batch composition. Check (E) fails the A/B templates for LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-Med, the B-mapped templates for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and LLaVA-1.5-13B, and the yes/no and A-mapped templates for Llama~3.2 Vision 11B on all datasets; the affected cells are recorded as ineligible. Two checkpoints answer ``yes'' to nearly every chest question (Gemma~3 4B: clean $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ on 94--97\% of rows), which the answerability grade records; three (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, LLaVA-Med) answer at chance.

\subsection{Coverage and cost}
\label{app:cf-coverage}

Nineteen checkpoints are complete on all three datasets with every module (the write matrix, calibration, the five additional templates, the dose sweep, the two refits, and the connector locus on NIH and COCO; the write matrix and calibration on CheXpert); Llama~3.2 Vision 11B is complete with its yes/no modules ineligible; Qwen2.5-VL-72B is complete on NIH; the 72B COCO/CheXpert blocks and Llama~3.2 Vision 90B were not run (compute budget), and InternVL3.5-38B is complete on all three datasets. The campaign consumed about 3,000 A100-hours; a chest block of a 7B checkpoint takes about ten GPU-hours.

\subsection{Robustness of the deficit}
\label{app:cf-robustness}

Every alternative direction is a vector $\boldsymbol u$ in the projected, train-scaled space and is lifted to the consumed space exactly as the logistic normal, $\hat\wvec=R(\boldsymbol u\oslash\max(\boldsymbol s,10^{-8}))$ normalised (Equation~\ref{eq:lift}). Because $x=R^{\top}\hvec$ with $R$ a fixed Gaussian matrix of near-orthonormal columns, a displacement $R\boldsymbol\delta$ of the consumed representation produces the displacement $R^{\top}R\boldsymbol\delta\approx\boldsymbol\delta$ of the projected features, so the same lift carries classifier normals, class-mean displacements, and patterns alike; the difference-of-means and pattern vectors are computed in the projected space and lifted once. The token-weighted variants scale the per-token dose $\alpha\lVert\hvec_{it}\rVert$ by a weight $w_t$ with mean one over the consumed tokens: the softmax variant keeps the L1 dose of the uniform write and the top-quarter variant ($w_t=4$ on the 25\% highest-scoring tokens) has twice its Frobenius norm.

\paragraph{Direction and label geometry.} Neither correlation among the six directions nor co-occurrence of the six report labels explains why a competing direction wins (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}). On NIH, the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $\cfGeoCosNih$ against $\cfGeoCosRandom$ for random pairs), and the labels are nearly uncorrelated (mean $\phi=\cfGeoPhiNih$). Yet NIH shows the same off-diagonal dominance as CheXpert, where directions and labels are correlated (mean cosine $\cfGeoCosChex$; Effusion--Consolidation $\cfGeoCosEffCons$, $\phi=\cfGeoPhiEffCons$). The own direction wins in \cfGeoOwnWinsNihPct\% of NIH and \cfGeoOwnWinsChexPct\% of CheXpert cells. The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction at chance rate (\cfGeoChanceCosMin{}--\cfGeoChanceCosMax\%) and the most co-occurring label at chance rate (\cfGeoChanceLabelMin{}--\cfGeoChanceLabelMax\%). Across the \cfGeoOffdiagCells{} chest off-diagonal cells, the competitor's advantage $W_{q,d}-W_{q,q}$ is uncorrelated with cosine (Spearman $\cfGeoRhoAdvCos$, $p=\cfGeoRhoAdvCosP$) and with $\phi$ ($\cfGeoRhoAdvPhi$). Removing each cell's strongest competitor does not restore ownership: \cfGeoTwoAbove{} of \cfGeoTwoAboveN{} chest cells (\cfGeoTwoAbovePct\%) have at least two competitors above the own write. The natural-image families with the highest co-occurrence (person with bicycle, $\phi=\cfGeoFamPhiPersonBicycle$; person with dog; chair with bottle) retain ownership in \cfGeoCocoFamMinPct{}--\cfGeoCocoFamMaxPct\% of cells when the competitor set is restricted to them. In the seed cell, Nodule is the \emph{least} similar of the five directions to Effusion (cosine $\cfSeedCosNodule$). It still moves the Effusion answer more than Effusion's own direction ($\cfSeedWNodule$ against $\cfSeedWEff$). The most similar direction, Atelectasis (cosine $\cfSeedCosAtel$), moves it by $\cfSeedWAtel$.

\paragraph{Scale, saturation, refits, and numerics.} The grades hold on the logit scale: ownership recomputed from the margin $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ agrees with the probability-scale verdict in \cfScaleAgree{} of \cfScaleAgreeN{} cells; every owned cell keeps $O^m_q>0$ and \cfScaleOwnedKeepFull{} of \cfScaleOwned{} keep the full verdict under the same rules (Table~\ref{tab:cf-scale}). Saturation runs counter to a ceiling explanation: COCO blocks have far more rows at $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ or $<0.01$ than chest blocks (median block share $\cfScaleSatCoco$ against $\cfScaleSatNih$ and $\cfScaleSatChex$). Restricting every cell to its unsaturated rows preserves $O_q>0$ in all \cfScaleUnsatOwned{} owned chest cells and in \cfScaleUnsatOtherPos{} of the \cfScaleUnsatOther{} others. The same pattern holds in the clean-No, clean-Yes, label-present, and label-absent strata. Refitting the probes on resampled training patients changes $O_q$ by a median of $\cfScaleRefitMedian$. The bf16 batch effects declared at the gates (candidate-logit differences of $\cfScaleBatchMin$--$\cfScaleBatchMax$) leave the verdicts unchanged: the margin sign agrees in \cfScaleBatchAgree{} of \cfScaleBatchN{} blocks, and the \cfScaleBatchDisagree{} disagreeing blocks contain no owned cell. Re-scoring the clean baseline inside a second module gives bitwise agreement in 100\% of rows for \cfScaleBaselineIdentical{} of \cfScaleBaselineN{} blocks and in \cfScaleBaselineRestPct\% for the rest, with $|\Delta P(\mathrm{yes})|>0.1$ in at most \cfScaleBaselineDpPct\% of rows. Writes at the connector locus are an order of magnitude smaller than at the primary locus (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $\cfConnWqqNih$ against $\cfPrimWqqNih$ on NIH and $\cfConnWqqCoco$ against $\cfPrimWqqCoco$ on COCO).

\subsection{Write structure across the grid}
\label{app:cf-figures}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure} condenses the $6\times6$ write matrices of every completed checkpoint. The top row is the cell-wise median matrix per dataset. The bottom row shows where the write goes: each written direction on the left is joined to the questions it moves on the right, with band width proportional to the mean positive answer change across checkpoints; bands that land on the writer's own question are coloured and bands that land elsewhere are grey. The share of the write that lands on its own question is \cfOwnShareNih\% on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert against \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO; on the chest sets the largest sinks are the Cardiomegaly question on NIH and the Edema and Effusion questions on CheXpert, which receive writes from every direction.

\subsection{Per-image examples}
\label{app:cf-examples}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-examples} shows the grades on single test images. For a chest example the pool is the test rows on which the finding is labelled present, the probe reads it (probability above $0.5$), and the clean answer is No; for a natural-image example the pool is the rows on which the probe reads the label correctly and the clean answer is No. Within the pool, rows are split by whether the concept's own write or the strongest competing write raises the answer more, and the row shown is the one nearest the median gap among rows where either write raises the answer by at least $0.2$; the last line of each example gives how many rows of the pool share its ordering, so that each example carries its own base rate.
