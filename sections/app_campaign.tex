\subsection{Acceptance decisions}
\label{app:cf-gates}

All 57 completed blocks pass checks (A), (B), (C), and (G) with zero determinism error, zero change at $\alpha=0$, and fp32-versus-model logit differences below 0.13. Check (D) exceeds the 0.25-logit tolerance for the Qwen3-VL, Gemma~3, MedGemma, InternVL, and Llama families (up to 1.84 logits for Gemma~3 12B); these are recorded as declared deviations and neutralised by the fixed batch composition. Check (E) fails the A/B templates for LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-Med, the B-mapped templates for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and LLaVA-1.5-13B, and the yes/no and A-mapped templates for Llama~3.2 Vision 11B on all datasets; the affected cells are recorded as ineligible. Two checkpoints answer ``yes'' to nearly every chest question (Gemma~3 4B: clean $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ on 94--97\% of rows), which the answerability grade records; three (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, LLaVA-Med) answer at chance.

\subsection{Coverage and cost}
\label{app:cf-coverage}

Nineteen checkpoints are complete on all three datasets with every module (the write matrix, calibration, the five additional templates, the dose sweep, the two refits, and the connector locus on NIH and COCO; the write matrix and calibration on CheXpert); Llama~3.2 Vision 11B is complete with its yes/no modules ineligible; Qwen2.5-VL-72B is complete on NIH; the 72B COCO/CheXpert blocks and Llama~3.2 Vision 90B were not run (compute budget), and InternVL3.5-38B is complete on NIH and COCO with its CheXpert block in progress at submission. The campaign consumed about 3,000 A100-hours; a chest block of a 7B checkpoint takes about ten GPU-hours.

\subsection{Write structure across the grid}
\label{app:cf-figures}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure} condenses the $6\times6$ write matrices of every completed checkpoint. The top row is the cell-wise median matrix per dataset. The bottom row shows where the write goes: each written direction on the left is joined to the questions it moves on the right, with band width proportional to the mean positive answer change across checkpoints; bands that land on the writer's own question are coloured and bands that land elsewhere are grey. The share of the write that lands on its own question is 24\% on NIH and 22\% on CheXpert against 79\% on COCO; on the chest sets the largest sinks are the Cardiomegaly question on NIH and the Edema and Effusion questions on CheXpert, which receive writes from every direction.

\subsection{Per-image examples}
\label{app:cf-examples}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-examples} shows the grades on single test images. For a chest example the pool is the test rows on which the finding is labelled present, the probe reads it (probability above $0.5$), and the clean answer is No; for a natural-image example the pool is the rows on which the probe reads the label correctly and the clean answer is No. Within the pool, rows are split by whether the concept's own write or the strongest competing write raises the answer more, and the row shown is the one nearest the median gap among rows where either write raises the answer by at least $0.2$; the last line of each example gives how many rows of the pool share its ordering, so that each example carries its own base rate.

A check on the write line means the concept's own direction raises its answer more than every competing direction on that image; a cross means a competitor raises it more. The first four chest examples are deficit cells of Qwen2.5-VL-7B; the next two are owned cells (Lingshu 32B Edema, Qwen2.5-VL-72B Atelectasis); the natural-image examples are owned cells of Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Lingshu 32B, and MedGemma 4B. The last line of each example counts the rows of its pool on which the same write wins.}
