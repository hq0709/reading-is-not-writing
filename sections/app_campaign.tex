\section{Campaign details}
\label{app:campaign}

This appendix lists the checkpoints and their consumed loci, the acceptance decisions, the coverage of the campaign, and the per-concept results of every completed block.

\subsection{Labels, registration, and artifact}
\label{app:cf-labels}

\paragraph{Labels.} Concept labels are the report-derived labels shipped with each dataset (NIH ChestX-ray14 finding labels; the CheXpert labeler output distributed with CheXpert Plus; COCO instance annotations). A row is positive for label 1, negative for label 0, and \emph{unknown} when the labeler returns uncertain or blank. We exclude unknown rows from probe fitting, selectivity, and answer AUROC. We write and score them like every other row. Cohorts are fixed manifests drawn with recorded seeds. Training and test cohorts are patient-disjoint. All cohorts are disjoint from every seed-study cohort. Table~\ref{tab:cf-prevalence} gives per-concept counts.

\paragraph{Registration.} The protocol was frozen before any grid block was scored. The loci, projection and probe settings, dose, reference family, six templates, module set, seven gates, and three decision rules are those of the registered protocol file and its code commit. The seed study's crossover and confirmation analyses were registered before they were run (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). One analysis pass over the packaged outcomes produces everything reported per block. The leaderboard, write-flow share, and per-image examples are descriptive summaries of these outputs, added after the grid was complete.

\paragraph{Artifact.} The release contains evaluation code and adapters, the registered protocol file, cohort manifests with their seeds, fitted directions, per-image outcomes for every module (write, dose, refit, locus, and template rows), every block's scorecard and coverage file, and rendered prompts. Adding a new checkpoint requires one adapter that names the block it consumes and passes the gates.

\subsection{Acceptance decisions}
\label{app:cf-gates}

All \cfWriteBlocks{} completed blocks pass checks (A), (B), (C), and (G) with zero determinism error, zero change at $\alpha=0$, and fp32-versus-model logit differences below 0.13. In check (D), the Qwen3-VL, Gemma~3, MedGemma, InternVL, and Llama families exceed the 0.25-logit tolerance (up to 1.84 logits for Gemma~3 12B). We record these as declared deviations and neutralise them through fixed batch composition. Check (E) fails for the A/B templates for LLaVA-1.5-7B and LLaVA-Med, the B-mapped templates for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and LLaVA-1.5-13B, and the yes/no and A-mapped templates for Llama~3.2 Vision 11B on all datasets. We record the affected cells as ineligible. Two checkpoints answer ``yes'' to nearly every chest question (Gemma~3 4B: clean $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ on 94--97\% of rows). The answerability grade records this behaviour. Three checkpoints (LLaVA-1.5 7B/13B, LLaVA-Med) answer at chance.

\subsection{Coverage and cost}
\label{app:cf-coverage}

Nineteen checkpoints are complete on all three datasets with every module (Table~\ref{tab:cf-coverage}): the write matrix, calibration, the five additional templates, the dose sweep, the two refits, and the connector locus on NIH and COCO; the write matrix and calibration on CheXpert. Llama~3.2 Vision 11B is complete with its yes/no modules ineligible. Qwen2.5-VL-72B is complete on NIH. The 72B COCO/CheXpert blocks and Llama~3.2 Vision 90B were not run (compute budget). InternVL3.5-38B is complete on all three datasets. The campaign consumed about 3,000 A100-hours; a chest block of a 7B checkpoint takes about ten GPU-hours.

\subsection{Robustness of the deficit}
\label{app:cf-robustness}

Every alternative direction is a vector $\boldsymbol u$ in the projected, train-scaled space. We lift it to the consumed space exactly as the logistic normal, by normalising $\hat\wvec=R(\boldsymbol u\oslash\max(\boldsymbol s,10^{-8}))$ (Equation~\ref{eq:lift}). Here $x=R^{\top}\hvec$, where $R$ is a fixed Gaussian matrix with near-orthonormal columns. A displacement $R\boldsymbol\delta$ of the consumed representation therefore produces the displacement $R^{\top}R\boldsymbol\delta\approx\boldsymbol\delta$ of the projected features. The same lift carries classifier normals, class-mean displacements, and patterns alike. We compute the difference-of-means and pattern vectors in the projected space and lift them once. The token-weighted variants scale the per-token dose $\alpha\lVert\hvec_{it}\rVert$ by a weight $w_t$ with mean one over the consumed tokens. The softmax variant preserves the L1 dose of the uniform write. The top-quarter variant ($w_t=4$ on the 25\% highest-scoring tokens) has twice the uniform write's Frobenius norm.

\paragraph{Direction and label geometry.} Neither correlations among the six directions nor co-occurrence of the six report labels explains why a competing direction wins (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}). On NIH, the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $\cfGeoCosNih$ against $\cfGeoCosRandom$ for random pairs). The labels are nearly uncorrelated (mean $\phi=\cfGeoPhiNih$). Yet NIH shows the same off-diagonal dominance as CheXpert, where directions and labels are correlated (mean cosine $\cfGeoCosChex$; Effusion--Consolidation $\cfGeoCosEffCons$, $\phi=\cfGeoPhiEffCons$). The own direction wins in \cfGeoOwnWinsNihPct\% of NIH and \cfGeoOwnWinsChexPct\% of CheXpert cells. The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction at chance rate (\cfGeoChanceCosMin{}--\cfGeoChanceCosMax\%) and the most co-occurring label at chance rate (\cfGeoChanceLabelMin{}--\cfGeoChanceLabelMax\%). Across the \cfGeoOffdiagCells{} off-diagonal chest cells, the competitor's advantage $W_{q,d}-W_{q,q}$ is uncorrelated with cosine (Spearman $\cfGeoRhoAdvCos$, $p=\cfGeoRhoAdvCosP$) and with $\phi$ ($\cfGeoRhoAdvPhi$). Removing each cell's strongest competitor does not restore ownership: \cfGeoTwoAbove{} of \cfGeoTwoAboveN{} chest cells (\cfGeoTwoAbovePct\%) have at least two competitors above the own write. The natural-image families with the highest co-occurrence (person with bicycle, $\phi=\cfGeoFamPhiPersonBicycle$; person with dog; chair with bottle) retain ownership in \cfGeoCocoFamMinPct{}--\cfGeoCocoFamMaxPct\% of cells when competitors are restricted to those families. Of the five directions in the seed cell, Nodule is the \emph{least} similar to Effusion (cosine $\cfSeedCosNodule$). It still moves the Effusion answer more than Effusion's own direction ($\cfSeedWNodule$ against $\cfSeedWEff$). The most similar direction, Atelectasis (cosine $\cfSeedCosAtel$), moves it by $\cfSeedWAtel$.

\paragraph{Scale, saturation, refits, and numerics.} The grades hold on the logit scale. Ownership recomputed from the margin $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ agrees with the probability-scale verdict in \cfScaleAgree{} of \cfScaleAgreeN{} cells. Every owned cell retains $O^m_q>0$, and \cfScaleOwnedKeepFull{} of \cfScaleOwned{} retain the full verdict under the same rules (Table~\ref{tab:cf-scale}). Saturation counters a ceiling explanation: COCO blocks have far more rows at $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ or $<0.01$ than chest blocks (median block share $\cfScaleSatCoco$ against $\cfScaleSatNih$ and $\cfScaleSatChex$). Restricting each cell to its unsaturated rows preserves $O_q>0$ in all \cfScaleUnsatOwned{} owned chest cells and in \cfScaleUnsatOtherPos{} of the \cfScaleUnsatOther{} others. The same pattern holds in the clean-No, clean-Yes, label-present, and label-absent strata. Refitting the probes on resampled training patients changes $O_q$ by a median of $\cfScaleRefitMedian$. The bf16 batch effects declared at the gates (candidate-logit differences of $\cfScaleBatchMin$--$\cfScaleBatchMax$) leave the verdicts unchanged. The margin sign agrees in \cfScaleBatchAgree{} of \cfScaleBatchN{} blocks; the \cfScaleBatchDisagree{} disagreeing blocks contain no owned cell. Re-scoring the clean baseline in a second module gives bitwise agreement in 100\% of rows for \cfScaleBaselineIdentical{} of \cfScaleBaselineN{} blocks and in at least \cfScaleBaselineRestPct\% for the rest, with $|\Delta P(\mathrm{yes})|>0.1$ in at most \cfScaleBaselineDpPct\% of rows. Writes at the connector locus are an order of magnitude smaller than those at the primary locus (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $\cfConnWqqNih$ against $\cfPrimWqqNih$ on NIH and $\cfConnWqqCoco$ against $\cfPrimWqqCoco$ on COCO).

\subsection{Write structure across the grid}
\label{app:cf-figures}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure} summarises the $6\times6$ write matrices of every completed checkpoint. The top row shows the cell-wise median matrix for each dataset. The bottom row traces each written direction on the left to the questions it moves on the right. Band width is proportional to the mean positive answer change across checkpoints. Bands reaching the writer's own question are coloured; bands reaching other questions are grey. The share of the write reaching its own question is \cfOwnShareNih\% on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert against \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO. On the chest sets, the largest sinks are the Cardiomegaly question on NIH and the Edema and Effusion questions on CheXpert. These questions receive writes from every direction.

\subsection{Per-image examples}
\label{app:cf-examples}

Figure~\ref{fig:cf-examples} shows grades on individual test images. For a chest example, the pool comprises test rows where the finding is labelled present, the probe reads it (probability above $0.5$), and the clean answer is No. For a natural-image example, the pool comprises rows where the probe reads the label correctly and the clean answer is No. Rows in each pool are split by whether the concept's own write or the strongest competing write raises the answer more. The row shown is nearest the median gap among rows where either write raises the answer by at least $0.2$. Each example's last line reports how many pool rows share its ordering, giving each example its own base rate.
