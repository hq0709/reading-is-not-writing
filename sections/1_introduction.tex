\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Linear probes are the standard tool for testing what a vision-language model (VLM) represents. They read out a concept by fitting a classifier to activations at a chosen layer \citep{alain2017understanding,belinkov2022probing}. In medical imaging, probes often succeed at linearly decoding clinical findings such as pleural effusion or cardiomegaly from the visual representation of general-purpose and medical VLMs \citep{zhu2026lost,pedersen2026probes}. Activation steering promises the converse: writing the probe direction back into the representation to change the model's behaviour \citep{turner2023steering,panickssery2024caa}. Together, these methods promise to make the probe direction a handle on the concept: read it to detect, write it to control. We ask whether that handle exists.

Showing that steering ``works'' does not answer the question. A write can change an answer for reasons unrelated to the concept's label. Any perturbation of matched size can push a yes/no answer. A direction fitted for one finding can carry a generic abnormality signal shared with other findings \citep{pahde2025navigating}. Random and sham directions test the first possibility. The second requires a comparison rarely made in steering studies: write every competing clinical direction at the same locus and dose in the same patients, then ask whether the concept's own direction moves the concept's own answer more than any other direction. We call the outcome \emph{ownership}. When a concept's probe direction is readable but unowned, that direction reads the concept and does not write it---reading is not writing.

We build an evaluation that tests this distinction at scale (Section~\ref{sec:framework}, Figure~\ref{fig:framework}). At the final visual block whose output the language model consumes, we assign each concept three grades. A concept is \emph{readable} if its probe beats matched random-label controls. It is \emph{answerable} if the model's clean yes/no answer beats chance. It is \emph{owned} if a fixed-dose write along its direction beats the 95th percentile of 119 random directions, a coordinate-permutation sham, and all five competing clinical directions under simultaneous max-$T$ bounds. Every NIH and COCO block reports dose response, refit stability, a second locus, label-shift controls, and prompt-template contrasts. We evaluate \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints from nine families (3B--72B; general-purpose and medical) on two chest-radiograph datasets with report-derived labels: NIH ChestX-ray14 and CheXpert Plus. We also evaluate them on COCO objects as a natural-image control (Section~\ref{sec:setup}).

The results show a consistent pattern (Section~\ref{sec:results}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, Figure~\ref{fig:overview}). Clinical concepts are read and answered in most cells but owned in \cfChestOwnedPct\%. Across all chest cells, a competing clinical direction moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does in \cfChestCompetitor{} cells. The seed result reproduces on new patients. The same procedure owns \cfCocoOwnedPct\% of cells on COCO, so the write itself is intact, and six small or fine-grained object categories are owned at the rate of the easy ones. Among the cells with a clean-answer AUROC of at least \cfStratTopEdge{}, \cfStratChestTopPct\% of chest cells are owned against \cfStratNatTopPct\% of natural-image cells. The gap is not task difficulty. A direction fitted to the model's own clean answer margin provides a handle where the probe direction does not.

\paragraph{Contributions.} (i) We introduce a concept-writability evaluation for VLMs that grades readability, answerability, and ownership at the consumed visual block. Benchmarks already score concept detection and steering as separate axes \citep{wu2025axbench}; what this evaluation adds is the matched clinical competitor, together with a random--sham reference family, simultaneous bounds, and registered acceptance gates. (ii) We report its first cross-model measurement: \cfCheckpoints{} checkpoints, nine families, three datasets, \cfCells{} scored concept cells. We report every control per block. (iii) Two findings hold across the grid. Clinical concepts are read and answered far more often than they are owned. The write mechanism is intact on natural objects and remains intact after matching chest and natural-image cells on difficulty. We release the evaluation, scorecards, and write matrices as a benchmark. A new model needs one adapter naming its consumed block.
