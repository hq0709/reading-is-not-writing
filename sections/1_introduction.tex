\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

A linear probe reads a concept from a representation; steering assigns it the role of writing that concept back. Under original yes/no prompts, Qwen's Effusion normal reproducibly beats random and sham controls, yet Nodule moves the same Effusion answer more. Mass's clinical advantage under \emph{show} A/B prompts reverses under \emph{is}. Clinical attribution depends on the direction--question--prompt combination.

Linear probes expose information available to a restricted readout family \citep{alain2017understanding,hewitt2019designing}, without establishing the model's use of it \citep{ravichander2021probing}. Random and sham vectors challenge generic steering effects. Semantic competition asks whether the named direction is privileged among equally constructed clinical directions for the named response.

For a fixed yes/no prompt, $W_{q,d}$ is the paired mean change in question $q$'s yes probability under direction $d$. Ownership $O_q=W_{q,q}-\max_{d\neq q}W_{q,d}$ compares the named direction with its strongest clinical competitor (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}). Contenders share readout construction, locus, dose, patients, and endpoint. Finding-present logit margins align answer mappings across prompts.

The original six gates establish the fixed-prompt reader--writer boundary on NIH ChestX-ray14. Three LLaVA/Qwen cells are readable, with positive controlled selectivity. LLaVA directions stop at random/sham controls. On 400 independent patients, Qwen Effusion's ownership contrast is $-0.0573$ (95\% CI $[-0.0694,-0.0450]$). A six-direction by six-question matrix yields zero owned concepts and no shared alias beyond 119 random directions. A 50-patient Consolidation test finds no reliable input-displacement closure: gain is 0.0022 versus a random maximum of 0.0025.

Both models evaluate the same 200 images from 100 fresh Consolidation patient pairs. Neither establishes positive natural paired opportunity (Section~\ref{sec:paired-opportunity}), motivating its measurement before interpreting component closure in a target population.

The answer-encoding crossover reveals mapping-odd dominance shared by random and sham directions and a prompt-dependent Mass ranking (Section~\ref{sec:encoding-results}). On 200 independent confirmation patients, Mass exceeds clinical competitors under both \emph{show} A/B prompts with positive simultaneous lower bounds. Larger effects among 119 random directions defeat both registered specificity criteria. Both \emph{is} coded margins are negative. Explicit \emph{show} yes/no has a similar observed margin.

Semantic competition formalizes the reader--writer problem; six gates separate steering efficacy, clinical attribution, and mechanism confirmation; independent prompt comparisons establish wording- and instruction-sensitive competition. Paired opportunity adds a behavioral boundary to component-closure interpretation.
