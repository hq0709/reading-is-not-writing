\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} reports every checkpoint's scores on every dataset. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows every concept cell across the three grades. Both chest datasets show the same pattern. Clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadable{} of \cfChestReadableN{} cells (\cfChestReadablePct\%): probe selectivity exceeds matched random-label controls by $0.1$--$0.3$ for most findings in most families. The models also answer: \cfChestAnswerable{} of the \cfChestAnswerableN{} cells with a scored write matrix (\cfChestAnswerablePct\%) have a clean-answer AUROC lower bound above chance. Of these answerable cells, \cfReadAns{} are also readable. Yet only \cfChestOwned{} cells (\cfChestOwnedPct\%) are owned. In \cfChestCompetitor{} cells (\cfChestCompetitorPct\%), the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does. Readability and answerability do not suffice for writing: only \cfReadAnsOwned{} of the \cfReadAns{} readable and answerable cells are owned (\cfReadAnsOwnedPct\%), compared with \cfRestOwned{} of the remaining \cfRestN{} (\cfRestOwnedPct\%).

Table~\ref{tab:cf-contingency} splits chest cells by whether they meet the steering reference and by the clinical comparison verdict. The steering reference requires the concept's own write to raise its answer above the 95th percentile of the random writes and above the absolute sham. Of the \cfChestAnswerableN{} chest cells, \cfChestRefMet{} meet this reference. Among these \cfChestRefMet{} cells, \cfChestOwned{} are owned, \cfChestRefStrong{} have a stronger competitor, and \cfChestRefUnres{} are unresolved. The remaining \cfChestRefBelow{} cells do not meet the steering reference and account for \cfChestBelowStrong{} of the \cfChestCompetitor{} stronger-competitor verdicts (\cfChestBelowStrongPct\%). In these cells, the concept's own write fails to clear the random and sham references. A direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more. The readable and answerable cells show the same pattern. Of the \cfReadAns{} readable and answerable cells, \cfReadAnsRefMet{} meet the steering reference. Of these, \cfReadAnsOwned{} are owned, \cfReadAnsRefStrong{} have a stronger competitor, and \cfReadAnsRefUnres{} are unresolved. Of their \cfReadAnsCompetitor{} stronger-competitor verdicts, \cfReadAnsBelowStrong{} occur in the \cfReadAnsRefBelow{} cells below the reference. On COCO, \cfCocoRefMet{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells meet the steering reference. Of these, \cfCocoOwned{} are owned. Of its \cfCocoCompetitor{} stronger-competitor verdicts, \cfCocoBelowStrong{} occur below the reference.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

We build on a two-model NIH study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). We reproduce the central result in a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}). The Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $\cfSeedWEff$. This effect exceeds the random 95th percentile ($\cfSeedEffRandP$) and sham ($\cfSeedEffSham$). It ranks second among the 120 compared effects. Yet the Nodule direction raises the Effusion answer by $\cfSeedWNodule$. Effusion ownership is $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=\cfSeedEffO$ (95\% CI $[\cfSeedEffOLo,\cfSeedEffOHi]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. We also reproduce prompt dependence for Mass. Effusion and Cardiomegaly are the most readable chest concepts (mean selectivity $\cfSelEffusion$ and $\cfSelCardiomegaly$ over \cfChestProbeBlocks{} chest blocks). Effusion is owned in \cfEffusionOwnedBlocks{} of \cfChestBlocks{} chest blocks. Of these owned blocks, \cfEffusionOwnedSmall{} have $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{The attribution deficit across the grid}
\label{sec:deficit}

Ownership is the rarest and least stable of the three grades on the chest sets. An effect floor of $0.05$ on both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$ leaves \cfChestFloored{} of the \cfChestOwned{} owned chest cells (\cfChestFlooredPct\% of \cfChestAnswerableN{}), compared with \cfCocoFloored{} of the \cfCocoOwned{} owned COCO cells (\cfCocoFlooredPct\%). Under both probe refits on resampled patients, \cfRefitGradeStableNih{} of the \cfRefitGradeOwnedNih{} owned NIH cells retain ownership, compared with \cfRefitGradeStableCoco{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedCoco{} on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-refit}). Clinical writes are not selective on the chest sets. To compute the write-flow share, we take the positive part of every entry of $W$, average it over checkpoints, and divide the sum of the six own-question entries by the total. The written concept's own question receives \cfOwnShareNih\% of the positive answer change on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert, compared with \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). This pattern is the attribution deficit. The model exposes the concept to a probe. The probe's direction is not a handle on the concept. Written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern in individual images. On a chest radiograph, the Effusion and Nodule writes raise the same answers. The competing write raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion write does. On a natural image, the dog write moves only the dog answer. The write matrix exposes two components of the deficit (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, concept writes in the chest datasets have little effect on their own answers. The median $W_{q,q}$ is $\cfMedianWqqNih$ on NIH and $\cfMedianWqqChex$ on CheXpert, compared with $\cfMedianWqqCoco$ on COCO. Second, competing directions share the effect whenever a chest write moves an answer (Table~\ref{tab:cf-contingency}). Across the grid, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO, the diagonal entry of $W$ dominates its row. On NIH and CheXpert, the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal. At every Qwen2.5-VL size, a competing direction raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does: Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, and Atelectasis at 72B.

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The same procedure produces the opposite result on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in \cfCocoOwned{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells (\cfCocoOwnedPct\%), with ownership margins of $\cfCocoMarginMin$--$\cfCocoMarginMax$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families. \cfCocoRefMet{} cells meet the steering reference. Only \cfCocoCompetitor{} cells have a stronger competitor. Of the \cfCocoBlocks{} checkpoints with a COCO block, \cfCocoBlocksFourPlus{} own at least four of the six objects. Dose response tracks the write. On COCO, median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $\cfDoseCocoLow$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $\cfDoseCocoPeak$ at $\alpha=+0.25$. It remains positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($\cfDoseCocoHalf$). On NIH, median ownership stays between $\cfDoseNihMin$ and $\cfDoseNihMax$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, direction construction, reference family, and decision rules therefore work as intended. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 shares one SigLIP tower across its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints. MedGemma shares one MedSigLIP tower across 4B and 27B. LLaVA-1.5 shares one CLIP tower across 7B and 13B. We intervene primarily at the tower's final block. Within each family, pooled features, fitted probes, and write vectors are therefore identical across sizes (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal within one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, with raw model-space cosines above $\cfPairsTowerCosMin$ between corresponding write vectors for every concept and pair). Readability is identical across sizes by construction. Every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} comes from how the connector and language model read the same written representation. The median paired difference is \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} times the refit standard deviation. It exceeds that standard deviation in \cfPairsRatioAboveOne{} of the \cfPairsRatioN{} pairs with a refit estimate (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). On NIH Effusion, the identical write on the same 600 patients produces a difference in $O_q$ of $\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelve$ $[\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelveLo,\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelveHi]$ between Gemma~3 4B and 12B. The difference between 12B and 27B is $\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySeven$ $[\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySevenLo,\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySevenHi]$. In \cfPairsFiveOrSix{} of the \cfPairsN{} shared-tower pair--dataset combinations, the paired patient-bootstrap interval for $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts. The median $|\Delta O_q|$ is \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} times the families' refit standard deviation (up to \cfPairsRatioSingleMax{} times for single concepts). In 12B, competing directions own the chest answers outright ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=\cfOGemmaTwelveEff$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=\cfOGemmaTwelveMass$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=\cfOGemmaTwelvePneu$). In 27B, no direction moves these answers ($\cfOGemmaTwentySevenEff$, $\cfOGemmaTwentySevenMass$, $\cfOGemmaTwentySevenPneu$). In 4B, the clean answer is yes on \cfGemmaFourYesMin--\cfGemmaFourYesMax\% of chest rows. Every own write lowers its answer's logit margin more than any competing write raises it ($O^m_q$ from $\cfPairsGemmaFourOmNear$ to $\cfPairsGemmaFourOmFar$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ in the same way (Pneumothorax $\cfOMedFourPneu$ versus $\cfOMedTwentySevenPneu$ and Edema $\cfOMedFourEdema$ versus $\cfOMedTwentySevenEdema$ on CheXpert). LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own no concepts on either chest set, yet own \cfLlavaSevenCoco{} and \cfLlavaThirteenCoco{} COCO objects with the same write. The chest cells owned by these readers (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) carry the least evidential weight. The reader effect spans the whole matrix, regardless of which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{The direction the reader responds to}
\label{sec:ansdir}

The same representation contains the reader's handle. For each question, we use ridge regression on the same features to fit the model's own clean answer margin (3{,}000 training rows). We lift the fitted direction as we do a probe normal and write it at the same dose against the same reference family (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). This answer direction $a_q$ is a handle where the label direction is not. Across the \cfAnsChestN{} chest cells in the \cfAnsBlocks{} scored blocks (Table~\ref{tab:cf-rescue}), $a_q$ is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedA{} and the label direction in \cfAnsChestOwnedLabel{}. Its write beats the strongest logistic competitor in \cfAnsChestBeats{}. Across the five held-out templates, $a_q$ remains owned in \cfAnsdirtChestKept{} of \cfAnsdirtChestKeptN{} chest and \cfAnsdirtCocoKept{} of \cfAnsdirtCocoKeptN{} COCO (cell, template) pairs (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdirt}). Across all pairs, regardless of whether their cell is owned under the primary template, $a_q$ is owned in \cfAnsdirtChestOwnedPairs{} of \cfAnsdirtChestPairsAll{} chest and \cfAnsdirtCocoOwnedPairs{} of \cfAnsdirtCocoPairsAll{} COCO (cell, template) pairs. In Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it is owned for \cfAnsQwenOwnedNih{} of six NIH concepts (Effusion $O^a=\cfAnsQwenEffO$ with an own write of $\cfAnsQwenEffW$) and all \cfAnsQwenOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. In Lingshu 32B, it is owned for \cfAnsLingOwnedNih{} NIH and \cfAnsLingOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. In Gemma~3 12B, it is owned for \cfAnsGemOwnedNih{} NIH and \cfAnsGemOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. The raw model-space cosine between the answer direction and the probe normal, taken as the median over the six concepts of a block, is $\cfAnsCosQwenMin$--$\cfAnsCosQwenMax$ across the two chest blocks of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and $\cfAnsCosLingMin$--$\cfAnsCosLingMax$ across those of Lingshu 32B, compared with $\cfAnsCosCocoMin$--$\cfAnsCosCocoMax$ across the COCO blocks. The cosine whitened by the training-feature covariance in the projected, train-scaled space, taken as the median over the cells of a dataset, is $\cfValCosSigmaChestMin$--$\cfValCosSigmaChestMax$ on the two chest sets and $\cfValCosSigmaCoco$ on COCO. The answer direction predicts the label less accurately than the probe. It predicts the model's own answer more accurately (Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}). On natural images, the readable and writable directions coincide. On chest radiographs, the model answers along a direction the probe does not read.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}

Ownership on the chest sets is concentrated among a few readers and varies by concept and dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns no concepts at 3B or 7B. At 32B, it owns Cardiomegaly. At 72B, it owns Atelectasis ($O=\cfOQwenSeventyTwoAtel$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass. Effusion is anti-owned at every size. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B. At 32B, it owns five, the highest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at every size. Its writes remain within $\cfInternvlWqqMax$ of zero on the chest sets. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts. It answers none above chance. It owns none.

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

The reference family and additional controls behave as designed across the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). Owned concept writes rank 1--6 among the 120 compared effects. The concept write typically ranks in the top decile on the chest sets.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Geometry, scale, saturation, refits, numerics.} No measurement alternative explains the deficit (Tables~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}--\ref{tab:cf-validation}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-robustness}). The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction or the most co-occurring label at chance rate. Logit-scale verdicts agree in \cfScaleAgree{} of \cfScaleAgreeN{} cells. COCO blocks are more saturated than chest blocks. Restricting each cell to unsaturated rows preserves the full owned grade in \cfScaleUnsatGradeKept{} of \cfScaleUnsatGradeN{} owned chest cells, with the steering reference recomputed on those rows and the simultaneous max-$T$ advantage evaluated over the same six-direction family. Refits change $O_q$ by a median of \cfScaleRefitMedian{}. The column-selectivity index $S_d=W_{d,d}/\sum_q|W_{q,d}|$ divides a direction's signed effect on its own question by its summed absolute effect on the six questions. Its median over cells is \cfValColSelNih{} on NIH and \cfValColSelChex{} on CheXpert, versus \cfValColSelCoco{} on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}). $S_d$ keeps negative effects and weights every cell equally, so it differs from the write-flow share of Section~\ref{sec:deficit}, which keeps only positive effects and pools them over checkpoints. On CheXpert, rows with a known label preserve the sign of $O_q$ in \cfValKnownSignAgree{} of \cfValKnownN{} cells. Radiologist labels for \cfValidRows{} CheXpert films reproduce ownership in \cfValidOwnedAgree{} of \cfValidCells{} cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-valid}). Rescoring in fp32 and at batch size one changes $W$ by at most \cfPrecisionMaxDW{} and alters \cfPrecisionGradeChanges{} of \cfPrecisionGradeCells{} grades (Table~\ref{tab:cf-precision}).

\paragraph{Alternative direction constructions.} The deficit is not specific to the logistic normal. Four alternative directions use the same projected, train-scaled features and write protocol (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern $\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta_c$ \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, the normal orthogonalised against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualised against the other five labels. Across the \cfAltdirBlocks{} scored blocks (\cfAltdirBlocksPerDs{} per dataset), each construction owns \cfAltdirChestPctMin{}--\cfAltdirChestPctMax\% of chest cells and \cfAltdirCocoPctMin{}--\cfAltdirCocoPctMax\% of COCO cells. Across those blocks, the absolute raw model-space cosine between the difference-of-means or pattern direction and the logistic normal, taken as the median over the six concepts of a block, ranges from $\cfAltdirCosMin$ to $\cfAltdirCosMax$. Each of these two directions owns more NIH cells and fewer COCO cells than the logistic normal. Lifted as displacements, these directions respectively own \cfAltdirdChestDom{} and \cfAltdirdChestPattern{} of \cfAltdirdChestN{} chest cells and \cfAltdirdCocoDom{} and \cfAltdirdCocoPattern{} of \cfAltdirdCocoN{} COCO cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdird}). Every construction preserves the chest-to-COCO gap. At least one construction owns \cfAltdirNihAny{} of \cfAltdirNihN{} cells on NIH and \cfAltdirChexAny{} of \cfAltdirChexN{} on CheXpert, compared with \cfAltdirCocoAny{} of \cfAltdirCocoN{} on COCO. Extending the clinical family to every additional dataset label with enough support retains \cfExtcompRetained{} of the \cfExtcompOwned{} owned cells in the \cfExtcompBlocks{} scored blocks (Table~\ref{tab:cf-extcomp}). Projection, sex, and age join the findings in one nine-direction family. These three are non-clinical properties of the same radiographs. Their probes read them at least as well as the clinical probes read the findings. Median probe AUROC is \cfAttrAurocMin{}--\cfAttrAurocMax{} per attribute, compared with \cfAttrClinAuroc{} for findings in the same blocks. All \cfAttrReadable{} of \cfAttrChestAttrN{} attribute cells are readable. The attributes own \cfAttrChestAttrOwned{} of \cfAttrChestAttrN{} cells; findings own \cfAttrChestClinOwned{} of \cfAttrChestClinN{}. On NIH, ownership is \cfAttrNihAttrOwned{}/\cfAttrNihAttrN{} for attributes and \cfAttrNihClinOwned{}/\cfAttrNihClinN{} for findings (Table~\ref{tab:cf-attr}). We weight writes along the logistic directions by the softmax of token probe scores or restrict writes to the top quarter of tokens at four times the weight (twice the Frobenius norm of the uniform write). These writes own \cfTokenwChestOwned{} of \cfTokenwChestN{} chest cells and \cfTokenwCocoOwned{} of \cfTokenwCocoN{} COCO cells in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B (Table~\ref{tab:cf-tokenw}). Concentrating the write where the probe reads does not create a clinical handle.
