\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} scores every checkpoint on every dataset and Figure~\ref{fig:overview} places every concept cell on the three grades. At a high level, the two chest datasets tell the same story. Clinical concepts are readable in 134 of 240 cells (56\%): probe selectivity reaches $0.1$--$0.3$ over matched random-label controls for most findings in most families. The models also answer: 140 of the 228 cells with a scored write matrix (61\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance, and 91 cells are both readable and answerable. Yet only 27 cells (12\%) are owned. In 150 cells (66\%) the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than the concept's own direction---and reading and answering are far from sufficient for writing: among the 91 cells that are readable and answerable, 19 are owned (21\%), against 8 of the remaining 137 (6\%). Ownership is also the least stable and the smallest of the three grades where it occurs on the chest sets: with an effect floor of $0.05$ on both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$, 18 of the 27 owned chest cells remain (8\% of 228) against 78 of the 100 owned COCO cells (68\%), and 6 of the 10 owned NIH cells keep $O_q>0$ when the probes are refitted on resampled patients, against 94 of 100 on COCO. The write itself is not selective on the chest sets: of the answer change a clinical write produces across the six questions, 24\% lands on its own question on NIH and 22\% on CheXpert, against 79\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). We call this pattern the attribution deficit: the model exposes the concept to a probe, but the probe's direction is not the concept's handle; written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern on single images: on a chest radiograph the Effusion write and the Nodule write raise the same set of answers, and the competitor raises the Effusion answer more; on a natural image the dog write moves only the dog answer. The deficit has two parts, and the write matrix separates them (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, on the chest sets the concept write moves its own answer little: the median $W_{q,q}$ is $0.015$ on NIH and $0.024$ on CheXpert against $0.270$ on COCO, and the steering reference---a write that beats the random family and the sham---is met in 41 of 228 chest cells against 102 of 114 COCO cells. Second, where the chest write does move the answer, the movement is shared: in 14 of those 41 cells a competing clinical direction moves the answer as much or more, and across the grid the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO the diagonal of $W$ dominates its row; on NIH and CheXpert the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal, and in every Qwen2.5-VL size a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation grew out of a two-model study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B on NIH (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). Its central result reproduces on a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}): the Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$, above the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and the sham ($0.017$), ranking second among the 120 compared effects; the Nodule direction raises it by $0.262$; and $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. All six diagonal contrasts are negative, as in the original ownership matrix, and the Mass prompt dependence reproduces: Mass is owned under the \emph{show} A/B templates and not under \emph{is}. Effusion, with Cardiomegaly the most readable chest concept (mean selectivity $0.17$ and $0.19$ over 40 chest blocks), is owned in 5 of 38 chest blocks across the grid, four of them with $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$ (Table~\ref{tab:cf-own-nih} and Table~\ref{tab:cf-own-chexpert}).

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The identical procedure on COCO gives the opposite outcome (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in 100 of 114 cells (88\%) with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families; 102 cells meet the steering reference and only six have a stronger competitor. Seventeen of nineteen checkpoints with a COCO block own at least four of the six objects; the two exceptions, LLaVA-1.5-13B (two) and LLaVA-Med (one), read the objects ($S=0.10$--$0.15$) and answer them. Dose response follows the write: the median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $-0.03$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $+0.26$ at $\alpha=+0.25$ and stays positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($+0.18$) on COCO, while on NIH it stays within $\pm0.1$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, the direction construction, the reference family, and the decision rules therefore behave as intended; the deficit belongs to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 ships one SigLIP tower for its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints, MedGemma one MedSigLIP tower for 4B and 27B, and LLaVA-1.5 one CLIP tower for 7B and 13B. Because the primary locus is that tower's final block, the pooled features, the fitted probes, and the write vectors are identical across sizes within each family (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal to one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is therefore the same across sizes by construction, and every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} is a difference in how the connector and language model read the same written representation. The differences are large against every source of noise in the grid (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). With the identical write and the same 600 patients, Gemma~3 4B and 12B differ in $O_q$ by $-0.83$ $[-0.85,-0.81]$ on NIH Effusion and 12B and 27B by $+0.79$ $[+0.76,+0.82]$; across the twelve shared-tower pair--dataset combinations the paired patient-bootstrap interval of $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts in eleven of them, and the median $|\Delta O_q|$ is 1.4--3.7 times the families' refit standard deviation (up to 8.8 times for single concepts). In 12B the competing directions own the chest answers outright ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.83$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=-0.65$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=-0.71$); in 27B no direction moves them ($-0.04$, $-0.08$, $-0.01$); in 4B the clean answer is yes on 94--97\% of chest rows and every own write lowers its own answer's logit margin more than any competitor raises it ($O^m_q$ from $-1.1$ to $-19.8$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ the same way (Pneumothorax $-0.60$ against $-0.06$ and Edema $+0.10$ against $-0.28$ on CheXpert), and LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own nothing on either chest set while owning five and two COCO objects with the same write. The owned chest cells of these readers (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) are the least of this evidence: the reader effect is in the whole matrix, not in which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}


Ownership on the chest sets is concentrated in a few readers and is concept- and dataset-specific (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns nothing at 3B and 7B, Cardiomegaly at 32B, and Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass at 72B, with Effusion anti-owned at every size. Qwen3-VL owns nothing at 4B and 8B on NIH and Nodule at 32B, and owns Consolidation and Edema at 4B and Edema at 32B on CheXpert. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns Cardiomegaly on NIH at 7B and adds Pneumothorax at 32B, and owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B---the largest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers all six NIH questions at 8B, 14B, and 38B but its writes stay within $0.04$ of zero on the chest sets at every size (the 38B owns Effusion and Consolidation on CheXpert with margins of $0.02$ and $0.01$), while it owns six (8B), five (14B), and six (38B) COCO objects with small margins. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts, answers none above chance, and owns none; LLaVA-Med answers Edema and Effusion on CheXpert and owns Effusion there with a margin of $0.003$. What a checkpoint owns on NIH does not transfer to CheXpert: Qwen2.5-VL-32B owns Cardiomegaly on NIH and anti-owns it on CheXpert ($O=-0.19$), and no checkpoint owns the same concept on both chest sets except Lingshu-32B (Cardiomegaly).

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

The reference family and the additional controls behave as designed across the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}, Appendix~\ref{app:campaign}). Owned concept writes sit beyond the random family and the sham, with ranks of 1--6 among the 120 compared effects; on the chest sets the concept write typically ranks in the top decile as well, which is why the deficit is one of attribution rather than efficacy. Relabelling the test rows within type shifts the answer far more than the concept write on COCO (label-shift gaps of $-3$ to $-23$ in 13 of 19 blocks) and by less than one in 14 of 20 NIH blocks. Wording and mapping contrasts are small for most families and large for Mass in Qwen2.5-VL and for bottle in Gemma~3 4B, reproducing the prompt dependence of the seed study.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Direction and label geometry.} Two alternatives to the reading of Section~\ref{sec:headline} are that a competing direction wins because the six directions are correlated, or because the six report labels co-occur. Both are measured (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}). On NIH the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $0.019$ against $0.023$ for random pairs) and the labels are nearly uncorrelated (mean $\phi=0.026$), yet NIH shows the same off-diagonal dominance as CheXpert, where directions and labels are correlated (mean cosine $0.24$; Effusion--Consolidation $0.51$, $\phi=0.74$): the own direction wins in 26\% of NIH cells and 29\% of CheXpert cells. Across the grid the strongest competitor is the most similar direction at chance rate (19--23\% against 20\%) and the most co-occurring label at chance rate (17--25\%), and over the 1{,}140 chest off-diagonal cells the competitor's advantage $W_{q,d}-W_{q,q}$ is uncorrelated with cosine (Spearman $0.05$, $p=0.11$) and with $\phi$ ($0.003$, $p=0.93$). Removing each cell's strongest competitor does not restore ownership: 126 of 228 chest cells (55\%) have at least two competitors above the own write and 81 (36\%) at least three, so a second competitor takes the place of the first. The natural-image families with the highest co-occurrence (person with bicycle, $\phi=0.14$; person with dog; chair with bottle) keep ownership in 98--100\% of cells when the competitor set is restricted to them. In the seed cell, Nodule is the \emph{least} similar of the five directions to Effusion (cosine $-0.06$, five co-positive patients of 600) and still moves the Effusion answer more than Effusion's own direction ($0.262$ against $0.190$), while the most similar direction, Atelectasis (cosine $0.49$), moves it by $0.083$.

\paragraph{Scale, saturation, refits, and numerics.} The same grades hold when the answer is measured on the logit scale instead of the probability scale: ownership recomputed from the margin $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ agrees with the probability-scale verdict in 335 of 342 cells, and every owned cell stays owned (Table~\ref{tab:cf-scale}). Saturation runs the wrong way for a ceiling explanation: COCO blocks have far more rows at $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ or $<0.01$ than chest blocks (median block share $0.82$ against $0.18$ on NIH and $0.32$ on CheXpert), and restricting every cell to its unsaturated rows keeps $O_q>0$ in all 27 owned chest cells and in 32 of the 191 other chest cells, with the same picture in the clean-No, clean-Yes, label-present, and label-absent strata. Refitting the probes on resampled training patients moves $O_q$ by a median of $0.03$; 94 of the 100 owned COCO cells and 6 of the 10 owned NIH cells keep $O_q>0$ under both refits, so clinical ownership, where it occurs, is fragile as well as rare. The bf16 batch effects declared at the gates (candidate-logit differences of $0.07$--$1.84$ between batched and single forwards) do not reach the verdicts: the margin sign agrees in 55 of 61 blocks, the six disagreeing blocks contain no owned cell, and the clean baseline re-scored inside a second module agrees to the bit in 100\% of rows for 22 of 33 blocks and in 75\% for the rest, with $|\Delta P(\mathrm{yes})|>0.1$ in at most 1.9\% of rows. Writes at the connector locus are an order of magnitude smaller than at the primary locus (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $0.009$ against $0.046$ on NIH and $0.008$ against $0.270$ on COCO), which is why the primary locus is the locus of the benchmark.
