\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} reports scores for every checkpoint on every dataset. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows every concept cell across the three grades. Both chest datasets show the same pattern. Clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadable{} of \cfChestReadableN{} cells (\cfChestReadablePct\%): probe selectivity exceeds matched random-label controls by $0.1$--$0.3$ for most findings in most families. The models also answer: \cfChestAnswerable{} of the \cfChestAnswerableN{} cells with a scored write matrix (\cfChestAnswerablePct\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance. Of these answerable cells, \cfReadAns{} are also readable. Yet only \cfChestOwned{} cells (\cfChestOwnedPct\%) are owned. In \cfChestCompetitor{} cells (\cfChestCompetitorPct\%), the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does. Readability and answerability do not suffice for writing: only \cfReadAnsOwned{} of the \cfReadAns{} readable and answerable cells are owned (\cfReadAnsOwnedPct\%), compared with \cfRestOwned{} of the remaining \cfRestN{} (\cfRestOwnedPct\%). Ownership is the rarest and least stable of the three grades on the chest sets. An effect floor of $0.05$ on both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$ leaves \cfChestFloored{} of the \cfChestOwned{} owned chest cells (\cfChestFlooredPct\% of \cfChestAnswerableN{}), compared with \cfCocoFloored{} of the \cfCocoOwned{} owned COCO cells (\cfCocoFlooredPct\%). Under both probe refits on resampled patients, \cfRefitGradeStableNih{} of the \cfRefitGradeOwnedNih{} owned NIH cells retain ownership, compared with \cfRefitGradeStableCoco{} of \cfRefitGradeOwnedCoco{} on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-refit}). Clinical writes are not selective on the chest sets. Of a clinical write's answer change across the six questions, \cfOwnShareNih\% lands on its own question on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert, compared with \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). We call this pattern the attribution deficit. The model exposes the concept to a probe. The probe's direction is not a handle on the concept. Written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern in individual images. On a chest radiograph, the Effusion and Nodule writes raise the same answers. The competing write raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion write does. On a natural image, the dog write moves only the dog answer. The write matrix reveals two components of the deficit (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, concept writes in the chest datasets have little effect on their own answers. The median $W_{q,q}$ is $\cfMedianWqqNih$ on NIH and $\cfMedianWqqChex$ on CheXpert, compared with $\cfMedianWqqCoco$ on COCO. Writes meet the steering reference---beating the random family and the sham---in \cfChestRefMet{} of \cfChestAnswerableN{} chest cells, versus \cfCocoRefMet{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} COCO cells. Second, when a chest write moves an answer, competing directions share its effect. Of those \cfChestRefMet{} cells, \cfChestRefStrong{} have a stronger competitor and \cfChestRefUnres{} are unresolved (Table~\ref{tab:cf-contingency}). Across the grid, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO, the diagonal entry of $W$ dominates its row. On NIH and CheXpert, the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal. At every Qwen2.5-VL size, a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation builds on a two-model NIH study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). We reproduce the central result in a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}). The Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$. This effect exceeds the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and sham ($0.017$). It ranks second among the 120 compared effects. Yet the Nodule direction raises the Effusion answer by $0.262$. Effusion ownership is $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. Prompt dependence for Mass also reproduces. Effusion and Cardiomegaly are the most readable chest concepts (mean selectivity $\cfSelEffusion$ and $\cfSelCardiomegaly$ over \cfChestProbeBlocks{} chest blocks). Effusion is owned in \cfEffusionOwnedBlocks{} of \cfChestBlocks{} chest blocks. Among these owned blocks, \cfEffusionOwnedSmall{} have $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The same procedure yields the opposite result on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in \cfCocoOwned{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells (\cfCocoOwnedPct\%), with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families. \cfCocoRefMet{} cells meet the steering reference. Only \cfCocoCompetitor{} cells have a stronger competitor. Among the \cfCocoBlocks{} checkpoints with a COCO block, \cfCocoBlocksFourPlus{} own at least four of the six objects. Dose response tracks the write. On COCO, median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $-0.03$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $+0.26$ at $\alpha=+0.25$ and remains positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($+0.18$). On NIH, median ownership stays within $\pm0.1$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, direction construction, reference family, and decision rules therefore work as intended. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 shares one SigLIP tower across its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints. MedGemma shares one MedSigLIP tower across 4B and 27B. LLaVA-1.5 shares one CLIP tower across 7B and 13B. We intervene primarily at the tower's final block. Within each family, pooled features, fitted probes, and write vectors are therefore identical across sizes (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal within one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is identical across sizes by construction. The connector and language model determine every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} by how they read the same written representation. The median paired difference is \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} times the refit standard deviation. It exceeds that standard deviation in \cfPairsRatioAboveOne{} of the \cfPairsRatioN{} pairs with a refit estimate (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). With the identical write on the same 600 patients, Gemma~3 4B and 12B differ in $O_q$ by $\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelve$ $[\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelveLo,\cfPairDeltaEffFourTwelveHi]$ on NIH Effusion. The difference between 12B and 27B is $\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySeven$ $[\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySevenLo,\cfPairDeltaEffTwelveTwentySevenHi]$. In \cfPairsFiveOrSix{} of the \cfPairsN{} shared-tower pair--dataset combinations, the paired patient-bootstrap interval for $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts. The median $|\Delta O_q|$ is \cfPairsRatioMin{}--\cfPairsRatioMax{} times the families' refit standard deviation (up to \cfPairsRatioSingleMax{} times for single concepts). In 12B, competing directions own the chest answers outright ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=\cfOGemmaTwelveEff$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=\cfOGemmaTwelveMass$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=\cfOGemmaTwelvePneu$). In 27B, no direction moves these answers ($\cfOGemmaTwentySevenEff$, $\cfOGemmaTwentySevenMass$, $\cfOGemmaTwentySevenPneu$). In 4B, the clean answer is yes on 94--97\% of chest rows. Every own write lowers its own answer's logit margin more than any competing write raises it ($O^m_q$ from $\cfPairsGemmaFourOmNear$ to $\cfPairsGemmaFourOmFar$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ in the same way (Pneumothorax $\cfOMedFourPneu$ versus $\cfOMedTwentySevenPneu$ and Edema $\cfOMedFourEdema$ versus $\cfOMedTwentySevenEdema$ on CheXpert). LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own no concepts on either chest set, yet own \cfLlavaSevenCoco{} and \cfLlavaThirteenCoco{} COCO objects with the same write. The chest cells these readers own (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) carry the least evidential weight. The reader effect spans the whole matrix, regardless of which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}

On the chest sets, ownership is concentrated among a few readers and varies by concept and dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns no concepts at 3B or 7B. It owns Cardiomegaly at 32B. At 72B, it owns Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass. Effusion is anti-owned at every size. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B, the highest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at every size. Its writes on the chest sets remain within $0.04$ of zero. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts but answers none above chance. It owns none.

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

Across the grid, the reference family and additional controls behave as designed (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). Owned concept writes rank 1--6 among the 120 compared effects. On the chest sets, the concept write typically ranks in the top decile.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Geometry, scale, saturation, refits, numerics.} No measurement alternative explains the deficit (Tables~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}--\ref{tab:cf-validation}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-robustness}). The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction or the most co-occurring label at chance rate. Logit-scale verdicts agree in \cfScaleAgree{} of \cfScaleAgreeN{} cells. COCO blocks are more saturated than chest blocks. Restricting each cell to unsaturated rows preserves all \cfChestOwned{} owned chest cells. Refits change $O_q$ by a median of \cfScaleRefitMedian{}. Column selectivity is the share of a direction's absolute effect that falls on its own question. It is \cfValColSelNih{} on NIH and \cfValColSelChex{} on CheXpert, versus \cfValColSelCoco{} on COCO. On CheXpert, rows with a known label preserve the sign of $O_q$ in \cfValKnownSignAgree{} of \cfValKnownN{} cells. Radiologist labels on \cfValidRows{} CheXpert films reproduce ownership in \cfValidOwnedAgree{} of \cfValidCells{} cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-valid}). Rescoring in fp32 and at batch size one changes $W$ by at most \cfPrecisionMaxDW{} and alters \cfPrecisionGradeChanges{} of \cfPrecisionGradeCells{} grades (Table~\ref{tab:cf-precision}).

\paragraph{Alternative direction constructions.} The deficit is not specific to the logistic normal. Four alternative directions use the same projected, train-scaled features and write protocol (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern $\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta_c$ \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, the normal orthogonalised against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualised against the other five labels. Across the \cfAltdirBlocks{} scored blocks (\cfAltdirBlocksPerDs{} per dataset), each construction owns \cfAltdirChestPctMin{}--\cfAltdirChestPctMax\% of chest cells and \cfAltdirCocoPctMin{}--\cfAltdirCocoPctMax\% of COCO cells. The difference-of-means and pattern directions have cosines of $0.1$--$0.4$ with the logistic normal. Each owns more NIH cells and fewer COCO cells than the logistic normal. Lifted as displacements, these directions respectively own \cfAltdirdChestDom{} and \cfAltdirdChestPattern{} of \cfAltdirdChestN{} chest cells and \cfAltdirdCocoDom{} and \cfAltdirdCocoPattern{} of \cfAltdirdCocoN{} COCO cells (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdird}). Every construction preserves the chest-to-COCO gap. At least one construction owns \cfAltdirNihAny{} of \cfAltdirNihN{} cells on NIH and \cfAltdirChexAny{} of \cfAltdirChexN{} on CheXpert, compared with \cfAltdirCocoAny{} of \cfAltdirCocoN{} on COCO. Extending the clinical family to every additional dataset label with enough support retains 5 of the 6 owned cells in the 8 scored blocks (Table~\ref{tab:cf-extcomp}). Projection, sex, and age join the findings in one nine-direction family. The attributes own \cfAttrChestAttrOwned{} of \cfAttrChestAttrN{} cells; findings own \cfAttrChestClinOwned{} of \cfAttrChestClinN{}. On NIH, ownership is \cfAttrNihAttrOwned{}/\cfAttrNihAttrN{} for attributes and \cfAttrNihClinOwned{}/\cfAttrNihClinN{} for findings (Table~\ref{tab:cf-attr}). We weight writes along the logistic directions by the softmax of token probe scores or restrict writes to the top quarter of tokens at four times the weight (twice the Frobenius norm of the uniform write). These writes own 0 of 6 chest concepts and 6 of 6 COCO objects in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B (Table~\ref{tab:cf-tokenw}). Concentrating the write where the probe reads does not create a clinical handle.

\paragraph{The direction the reader responds to.} The same representation contains the reader's handle. For each question, we fit the model's own clean answer margin using ridge regression on the same features (3{,}000 training rows). We lift the fitted direction like a probe normal and write it at the same dose against the same reference family (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). This answer direction $a_q$ is a handle where the label direction is not. Across the \cfAnsChestN{} chest cells in the \cfAnsBlocks{} scored blocks (Table~\ref{tab:cf-rescue}), $a_q$ is owned in \cfAnsChestOwnedA{} and the label direction in \cfAnsChestOwnedLabel{}. Its write beats the strongest logistic competitor in \cfAnsChestBeats{}. Across the five held-out templates, $a_q$ remains owned in \cfAnsdirtChestKept{} of \cfAnsdirtChestKeptN{} chest and \cfAnsdirtCocoKept{} of \cfAnsdirtCocoKeptN{} COCO (cell, template) pairs (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdirt}). In Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it is owned for \cfAnsQwenOwnedNih{} of six NIH concepts (Effusion $O^a=\cfAnsQwenEffO$ with an own write of $\cfAnsQwenEffW$) and all \cfAnsQwenOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. In Lingshu 32B, it is owned for \cfAnsLingOwnedNih{} NIH and \cfAnsLingOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. In Gemma~3 12B, it is owned for \cfAnsGemOwnedNih{} NIH and \cfAnsGemOwnedChex{} CheXpert concepts. On the chest sets, its raw cosine with the probe normal is $\cfAnsCosQwenMin$--$\cfAnsCosQwenMax$ in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and $\cfAnsCosLingMin$--$\cfAnsCosLingMax$ in Lingshu 32B, compared with $\cfAnsCosCocoMin$--$\cfAnsCosCocoMax$ on COCO. Whitening by the training covariance gives $\cfValCosSigmaChestMin$--$\cfValCosSigmaChestMax$ on the chest sets and $\cfValCosSigmaCoco$ on COCO. The answer direction reads the label less accurately than the probe. It reads the model's own answer more accurately (Table~\ref{tab:cf-validation}). On natural images, the readable and writable directions coincide. On chest radiographs, the model answers along a direction the probe does not read.
