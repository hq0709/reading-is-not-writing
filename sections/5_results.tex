\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} scores every checkpoint on every dataset and Figure~\ref{fig:overview} places every concept cell on the three grades. At a high level, the two chest datasets tell the same story. Clinical concepts are readable in 129 of 234 cells (55\%): probe selectivity reaches $0.1$--$0.3$ over matched random-label controls for most findings in most families. The models also answer: 134 of the 222 cells with a scored write matrix (60\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance, and 86 cells are both readable and answerable. Yet only 27 cells (12\%) are owned. In 147 cells (66\%) the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than the concept's own direction---and readability does not predict ownership: among the 86 cells that are readable and answerable, 19 are owned. We refer to this pattern as the attribution deficit: the model exposes the concept to a reader and uses something else when the concept is written.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern on single images: on a chest radiograph the Effusion write and the Nodule write raise the same set of answers, and the competitor raises the Effusion answer more; on a natural image the dog write moves only the dog answer. The deficit has two parts, and the write matrix separates them (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, on the chest sets the concept write moves its own answer little: the median $W_{q,q}$ is $0.020$ on NIH and $0.024$ on CheXpert against $0.330$ on COCO, and the steering reference---a write that beats the random family and the sham---is met in 40 of 222 chest cells against 96 of 108 COCO cells. Second, where the chest write does move the answer, the movement is shared: in 13 of those 40 cells a competing clinical direction moves the answer as much or more, and across the grid the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO the diagonal of $W$ dominates its row; on NIH and CheXpert the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal, and in every Qwen2.5-VL size a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation grew out of a two-model study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B on NIH (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). Its central result reproduces on a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}): the Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$, above the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and the sham ($0.017$), ranking second among the 120 compared effects; the Nodule direction raises it by $0.252$; and $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. All six diagonal contrasts are negative, as in the original ownership matrix, and the Mass prompt dependence reproduces: Mass is owned under the \emph{show} A/B templates and not under \emph{is}. Effusion, the most readable chest concept, is owned in 5 of 37 chest blocks across the grid, four of them with $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$ (Table~\ref{tab:cf-own-nih} and Table~\ref{tab:cf-own-chexpert}).

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The identical procedure on COCO gives the opposite outcome (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in 94 of 108 cells (87\%) with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families; 96 cells meet the steering reference and only six have a stronger competitor. Sixteen of eighteen checkpoints with a COCO block own at least four of the six objects; the two exceptions, LLaVA-1.5-13B (two) and LLaVA-Med (one), read the objects ($S=0.10$--$0.15$) and answer them. Dose response is monotone: the median ownership across questions rises from zero at $\alpha=0$ to $0.4$--$0.9$ at $\alpha=+0.5$ on COCO, while on NIH it stays within $\pm0.1$ for most checkpoints and falls for the rest (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, the direction construction, the reference family, and the decision rules therefore behave as intended; the deficit belongs to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 ships one SigLIP tower for its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints, and MedGemma one MedSigLIP tower for 4B and 27B. Because the primary locus is that tower's final block, the pooled features, the fitted probes, and the write vectors are identical across sizes within each family (byte-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B; equal up to bf16 rounding for 27B and for MedGemma, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is therefore the same across sizes by construction, and every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} is a difference in how the connector and language model read the same written representation. With the same write, Gemma~3 4B owns nothing on either chest set, 12B owns Nodule on NIH and Edema on CheXpert, and 27B owns nothing; on COCO the three sizes own 5, 6, and 6 objects. MedGemma 4B owns Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert and 27B owns neither, while both own most COCO objects.

Ownership on the chest sets is concentrated in a few readers and is concept- and dataset-specific (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns nothing at 3B and 7B, Cardiomegaly at 32B, and Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass at 72B, with Effusion anti-owned at every size. Qwen3-VL owns nothing at 4B and 8B on NIH and Nodule at 32B, and owns Consolidation and Edema at 4B and Edema at 32B on CheXpert. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns Cardiomegaly on NIH at 7B and adds Pneumothorax at 32B, and owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B---the largest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers all six NIH questions at 8B and 14B but its writes stay within $0.04$ of zero on the chest sets, while it owns six (8B) and five (14B) COCO objects with small margins. LLaVA-1.5 and LLaVA-Med read the chest concepts, answer none above chance, and own none. What a checkpoint owns on NIH does not transfer to CheXpert: Qwen2.5-VL-32B owns Cardiomegaly on NIH and anti-owns it on CheXpert ($O=-0.19$), and no checkpoint owns the same concept on both chest sets except Lingshu-32B (Cardiomegaly).

\subsection{Controls}
\label{sec:controls}

The reference family and the additional controls behave as designed across the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}, Appendix~\ref{app:campaign}). Owned concept writes sit beyond the random family and the sham, with ranks of 1--6 among the 120 compared effects; on the chest sets the concept write typically ranks in the top decile as well, which is why the deficit is one of attribution rather than efficacy. Writes at the connector locus are close to inert: the connector-locus median ownership lies within $\pm0.03$ of zero in all but three NIH blocks (LLaVA-1.5-7B $-0.07$, Gemma~3 12B $-0.07$, Qwen2.5-VL-32B $-0.04$), while the primary-locus write moves answers by up to $0.9$. Refitting the directions with new seeds changes ownership by $0.003$--$0.08$ in most blocks and by $0.10$--$0.18$ in six (the Gemma family, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, and Qwen3-VL-8B on NIH). Relabelling the test rows within type shifts the answer far more than the concept write on COCO (label-shift gaps of $-3$ to $-23$ in 13 of 18 blocks) and by less than one in 13 of 19 NIH blocks. Wording and mapping contrasts are small for most families and large for Mass in Qwen2.5-VL and for bottle in Gemma~3 4B, reproducing the prompt dependence of the seed study.
