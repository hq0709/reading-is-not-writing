\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} reports scores for every checkpoint on every dataset. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows every concept cell across the three grades. Both chest datasets show the same pattern. Clinical concepts are readable in \cfChestReadable{} of \cfChestReadableN{} cells (\cfChestReadablePct\%): probe selectivity reaches $0.1$--$0.3$ over matched random-label controls for most findings in most families. The models also answer: \cfChestAnswerable{} of the \cfChestAnswerableN{} cells with a scored write matrix (\cfChestAnswerablePct\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance. Among these answerable cells, \cfReadAns{} are also readable. Yet only \cfChestOwned{} cells (\cfChestOwnedPct\%) are owned. In \cfChestCompetitor{} cells (\cfChestCompetitorPct\%), the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does. Readability and answerability do not suffice for writing: of the \cfReadAns{} readable and answerable cells, \cfReadAnsOwned{} are owned (\cfReadAnsOwnedPct\%), against \cfRestOwned{} of the remaining \cfRestN{} (\cfRestOwnedPct\%). Ownership is the least common and least stable of the three grades on the chest sets. Applying an effect floor of $0.05$ to both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$ leaves \cfChestFloored{} of the \cfChestOwned{} owned chest cells (\cfChestFlooredPct\% of \cfChestAnswerableN{}), against \cfCocoFloored{} of the \cfCocoOwned{} owned COCO cells (\cfCocoFlooredPct\%). Refitting the probes on resampled patients leaves 6 of the \cfNihOwned{} owned NIH cells with $O_q>0$, against 94 of \cfCocoOwned{} on COCO. Clinical writes are not selective on the chest sets. Of the answer change a clinical write produces across the six questions, \cfOwnShareNih\% lands on its own question on NIH and \cfOwnShareChex\% on CheXpert, against \cfOwnShareCoco\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). We call this pattern the attribution deficit. The model exposes the concept to a probe. The probe's direction is not a handle on the concept. Written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern in individual images. On a chest radiograph, the Effusion and Nodule writes raise the same answers. The competing write raises the Effusion answer more. On a natural image, the dog write moves only the dog answer. The write matrix reveals two parts of the deficit (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, concept writes have little effect on their own answers on the chest sets. The median $W_{q,q}$ is $\cfMedianWqqNih$ on NIH and $\cfMedianWqqChex$ on CheXpert, compared with $\cfMedianWqqCoco$ on COCO. Writes meet the steering reference---beating the random family and the sham---in \cfChestRefMet{} of \cfChestAnswerableN{} chest cells, against \cfCocoRefMet{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} COCO cells. Second, when a chest write moves an answer, that movement is shared. In \cfChestRefNotOwned{} of those \cfChestRefMet{} cells, a competing clinical direction moves the answer as much or more. Across the grid, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO, the diagonal entry of $W$ dominates its row. On NIH and CheXpert, the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal. At every Qwen2.5-VL size, a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation builds on a two-model NIH study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). The central result reproduces on a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}). The Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$, exceeding the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and sham ($0.017$). This effect ranks second among the 120 compared effects. The Nodule direction raises the Effusion answer by $0.262$. Effusion ownership is $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. Prompt dependence for Mass also reproduces. Effusion and Cardiomegaly are the most readable chest concepts (mean selectivity $\cfSelEffusion$ and $\cfSelCardiomegaly$ over \cfChestProbeBlocks{} chest blocks). Effusion is owned in \cfEffusionOwnedBlocks{} of \cfChestBlocks{} chest blocks. In \cfEffusionOwnedSmall{} of these, $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The identical procedure yields the opposite result on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in \cfCocoOwned{} of \cfCocoOwnedN{} cells (\cfCocoOwnedPct\%), with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families. \cfCocoRefMet{} cells meet the steering reference. Only \cfCocoCompetitor{} have a stronger competitor. \cfCocoBlocksFourPlus{} of \cfCocoBlocks{} checkpoints with a COCO block own at least four of the six objects. The two exceptions, LLaVA-1.5-13B (two) and LLaVA-Med (one), read the objects ($S=0.10$--$0.15$) and answer them. Dose response tracks the write. On COCO, median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $-0.03$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $+0.26$ at $\alpha=+0.25$. It remains positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($+0.18$). On NIH, it remains within $\pm0.1$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, direction construction, reference family, and decision rules therefore work as intended. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 shares one SigLIP tower across its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints. MedGemma shares one MedSigLIP tower across 4B and 27B. LLaVA-1.5 shares one CLIP tower across 7B and 13B. The primary intervention site is the tower's final block. Pooled features, fitted probes, and write vectors are therefore identical across sizes within each family (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal within one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is identical across sizes by construction. Every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} comes from how the connector and language model read the same written representation. These differences are large relative to every source of noise in the grid (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). With the identical write on the same 600 patients, Gemma~3 4B and 12B differ in $O_q$ by $-0.83$ $[-0.85,-0.81]$ on NIH Effusion. The difference between 12B and 27B is $+0.79$ $[+0.76,+0.82]$. The paired patient-bootstrap interval for $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts in eleven of the twelve shared-tower pair--dataset combinations. The median $|\Delta O_q|$ is 1.4--3.7 times the families' refit standard deviation (up to 8.8 times for single concepts). Competing directions own the chest answers outright in 12B ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.83$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=-0.65$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=-0.71$). In 27B, no direction moves these answers ($-0.04$, $-0.08$, $-0.01$). In 4B, the clean answer is yes on 94--97\% of chest rows. Every own write lowers its own answer's logit margin more than any competitor raises it ($O^m_q$ from $-1.1$ to $-19.8$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ the same way (Pneumothorax $-0.60$ against $-0.06$ and Edema $+0.10$ against $-0.28$ on CheXpert). LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own nothing on either chest set while owning five and two COCO objects with the same write. The chest cells these readers own (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) are the least of this evidence. The reader effect lies in the whole matrix, not in which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}

On the chest sets, ownership is concentrated in a few readers and varies by concept and dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns no concepts at 3B and 7B. It owns Cardiomegaly at 32B and Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass at 72B. Effusion is anti-owned at every size. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B, the largest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at 8B, 14B, and 38B. Its writes stay within $0.04$ of zero on the chest sets. It owns five or six COCO objects. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts but answers none above chance and owns none. Only Lingshu-32B owns the same concept (Cardiomegaly) on both chest sets.

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

Across the grid, the reference family and additional controls behave as designed (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). Owned concept writes rank 1--6 among the 120 compared effects. On the chest sets, the concept write also typically ranks in the top decile. The deficit has two parts: \cfChestRefBelow{} of \cfChestAnswerableN{} chest writes stay below the steering reference, and where the write does reach the answer a competitor reaches it as much or more in \cfChestRefNotOwned{} of \cfChestRefMet{} cells. The label gap, the margin shift under the concept's own write on label-positive rows minus that on label-negative rows, is $-3$ to $-23$ logits in 13 of \cfCocoBlocks{} COCO blocks (the write moves the negatives, whose clean answer is no) and within one logit in 14 of \cfNihBlocks{} NIH blocks. Wording and mapping contrasts are small for most families. Large contrasts for Mass in Qwen2.5-VL and bottle in Gemma~3 4B reproduce the seed study's prompt dependence.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Geometry, scale, saturation, refits, numerics.} No measurement alternative explains the deficit (Tables~\ref{tab:cf-geometry} and \ref{tab:cf-scale}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-robustness}). On NIH, the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $0.019$ against $0.023$). The labels are nearly uncorrelated ($\phi=0.026$). Yet the own direction wins in 26\% of NIH and 29\% of CheXpert cells. The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction or the most co-occurring label at chance rate. Removing this competitor leaves a second competitor above the own write in 55\% of chest cells. The most co-occurring COCO families retain ownership in 98--100\% of cells. The verdicts agree on the logit scale in 335 of 342 cells. COCO blocks are more saturated than chest blocks (median block share $0.82$ against $0.18$ and $0.32$). Restricting every cell to unsaturated rows preserves all 27 owned chest cells. Refits change $O_q$ by a median of $0.03$. The declared bf16 batch effects change no verdict (six disagreeing blocks, no owned cell among them). Connector-locus writes are an order of magnitude smaller than primary-locus writes (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $0.009$ against $0.046$ on NIH, $0.008$ against $0.270$ on COCO).

\paragraph{Alternative direction constructions.} The deficit is not a property of the logistic normal. We write four other directions built from the same projected, train-scaled features using the same protocol (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern $\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta_c$ \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, the normal orthogonalised against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualised against the other five labels. Across the \cfAltdirBlocks{} blocks scored so far (\cfAltdirBlocksPerDs{} per dataset), every construction owns \cfAltdirChestPctMin{}--\cfAltdirChestPctMax\% of chest cells (NIH \cfAltdirNihLogistic{}, \cfAltdirNihDom{}, \cfAltdirNihPattern{}, \cfAltdirNihOrth{}, and \cfAltdirNihResid{} of \cfAltdirNihN{}; CheXpert \cfAltdirChexLogistic{}, \cfAltdirChexDom{}, \cfAltdirChexPattern{}, \cfAltdirChexOrth{}, and \cfAltdirChexResid{} of \cfAltdirChexN{}) and \cfAltdirCocoPctMin{}--\cfAltdirCocoPctMax\% of COCO cells (\cfAltdirCocoLogistic{}, \cfAltdirCocoDom{}, \cfAltdirCocoPattern{}, \cfAltdirCocoOrth{}, and \cfAltdirCocoResid{} of \cfAltdirCocoN{}). The difference-of-means and pattern directions, at cosine $0.1$--$0.4$ to the logistic normal, add a few chest cells and lose many natural-object cells. They are weaker handles everywhere. At least one construction owns \cfAltdirNihAny{} of \cfAltdirNihN{} cells on NIH and \cfAltdirChexAny{} of \cfAltdirChexN{} on CheXpert, against \cfAltdirCocoAny{} of \cfAltdirCocoN{} on COCO. Enlarging the clinical family with every further dataset label that has enough support (Table~\ref{tab:cf-extcomp}) creates no ownership. Writing the logistic directions with per-token weights, by the softmax of each token's probe score or only on the top quarter of tokens at four times the weight (twice the Frobenius norm of the uniform write), owns 0 of 6 chest concepts and 6 of 6 COCO objects in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B (Table~\ref{tab:cf-tokenw}). Concentrating the write where the probe reads does not create a clinical handle.

\paragraph{The direction the reader responds to.} The reader's handle lies in the same representation. For each question, we fit a ridge regression of the model's own clean answer margin on the same features (3{,}000 training rows). We lift the fitted direction like a probe normal and write it at the same dose against the same reference family (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). This answer direction $a_q$ is a handle where the label direction is not. In Qwen2.5-VL-7B it is owned for three of six NIH concepts (Effusion $O^a=+0.29$ with an own write of $0.81$) and all six CheXpert concepts. In Lingshu 32B it is owned for three NIH and five CheXpert concepts. In every block, its write beats the strongest logistic competitor on all six questions. It also differs from the probe normal: their cosine is $0.06$--$0.07$ in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and $0.30$--$0.33$ in Lingshu 32B on the chest sets, against $0.54$--$0.64$ on COCO. On natural images, the readable and writable directions coincide. On chest radiographs, the model answers along a direction the probe does not read.
