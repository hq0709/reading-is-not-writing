\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} scores every checkpoint on every dataset, and Figure~\ref{fig:overview} places every concept cell on the three grades. Both chest datasets show the same pattern. Clinical concepts are readable in 134 of 240 cells (56\%): probe selectivity reaches $0.1$--$0.3$ over matched random-label controls for most findings in most families. The models also answer: 140 of the 228 cells with a scored write matrix (61\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance. Of these, 91 cells are both readable and answerable. Yet only 27 cells (12\%) are owned. In 150 cells (66\%), the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does. Reading and answering are far from sufficient for writing: of the 91 readable and answerable cells, 19 are owned (21\%), against 8 of the remaining 137 (6\%). Where ownership occurs on the chest sets, it is the smallest and least stable of the three grades. With an effect floor of $0.05$ on both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$, 18 of the 27 owned chest cells remain (8\% of 228), against 78 of the 100 owned COCO cells (68\%). When the probes are refitted on resampled patients, 6 of the 10 owned NIH cells keep $O_q>0$, against 94 of 100 on COCO. The write itself is not selective on the chest sets: of the answer change a clinical write produces across the six questions, 24\% lands on its own question on NIH and 22\% on CheXpert, against 79\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). We call this pattern the attribution deficit. The model exposes the concept to a probe, but the probe's direction is not the concept's handle. Written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern on single images. On a chest radiograph, the Effusion and Nodule writes raise the same set of answers. The competitor raises the Effusion answer more. On a natural image, the dog write moves only the dog answer. The write matrix separates the deficit into two parts (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, on the chest sets the concept write moves its own answer little. The median $W_{q,q}$ is $0.015$ on NIH and $0.024$ on CheXpert against $0.270$ on COCO. The steering reference---a write that beats the random family and the sham---is met in 41 of 228 chest cells against 102 of 114 COCO cells. Second, where the chest write moves the answer, the movement is shared. In 14 of those 41 cells, a competing clinical direction moves the answer as much or more. Across the grid, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO, the diagonal of $W$ dominates its row. On NIH and CheXpert, the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal. In every Qwen2.5-VL size, a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation grew out of a two-model study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B on NIH (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). Its central result reproduces on a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}). The Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$, above the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and sham ($0.017$), ranking second among the 120 compared effects. The Nodule direction raises it by $0.262$. Effusion ownership is $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. All six diagonal contrasts are negative, as in the original ownership matrix. The Mass prompt dependence also reproduces. Effusion and Cardiomegaly are the most readable chest concepts (mean selectivity $0.17$ and $0.19$ over 40 chest blocks). Effusion is owned in 5 of 38 chest blocks, four of them with $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The identical procedure gives the opposite outcome on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in 100 of 114 cells (88\%) with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families; 102 cells meet the steering reference and only six have a stronger competitor. Seventeen of nineteen checkpoints with a COCO block own at least four of the six objects. The two exceptions, LLaVA-1.5-13B (two) and LLaVA-Med (one), read the objects ($S=0.10$--$0.15$) and answer them. Dose response follows the write: on COCO, median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $-0.03$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $+0.26$ at $\alpha=+0.25$ and stays positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($+0.18$). On NIH, it stays within $\pm0.1$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, direction construction, reference family, and decision rules therefore behave as intended. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 uses one SigLIP tower for its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints, MedGemma one MedSigLIP tower for 4B and 27B, and LLaVA-1.5 one CLIP tower for 7B and 13B. The primary locus is that tower's final block, so pooled features, fitted probes, and write vectors are identical across sizes within each family (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal to one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is therefore the same across sizes by construction. Every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} comes from how the connector and language model read the same written representation. These differences are large relative to every source of noise in the grid (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). With the identical write and the same 600 patients, Gemma~3 4B and 12B differ in $O_q$ by $-0.83$ $[-0.85,-0.81]$ on NIH Effusion and 12B and 27B by $+0.79$ $[+0.76,+0.82]$. The paired patient-bootstrap interval of $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts in eleven of the twelve shared-tower pair--dataset combinations. The median $|\Delta O_q|$ is 1.4--3.7 times the families' refit standard deviation (up to 8.8 times for single concepts). In 12B the competing directions own the chest answers outright ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.83$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=-0.65$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=-0.71$). In 27B no direction moves them ($-0.04$, $-0.08$, $-0.01$). In 4B the clean answer is yes on 94--97\% of chest rows. Every own write lowers its own answer's logit margin more than any competitor raises it ($O^m_q$ from $-1.1$ to $-19.8$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ the same way (Pneumothorax $-0.60$ against $-0.06$ and Edema $+0.10$ against $-0.28$ on CheXpert). LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own nothing on either chest set while owning five and two COCO objects with the same write. The owned chest cells of these readers (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) are the least of this evidence: the reader effect is in the whole matrix, not in which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}

Ownership on the chest sets is concentrated in a few readers and is concept- and dataset-specific (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns nothing at 3B and 7B, Cardiomegaly at 32B, and Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass at 72B. It anti-owns Effusion at every size. Qwen3-VL owns Nodule at 32B on NIH and Consolidation, Edema (4B) and Edema (32B) on CheXpert. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B, the largest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at 8B, 14B, and 38B. Its writes on the chest sets stay within $0.04$ of zero at every size. On COCO, it owns five or six objects with small margins. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts, answers none above chance, and owns none. Checkpoint ownership does not transfer from NIH to CheXpert: only Lingshu-32B (Cardiomegaly) owns the same concept on both.

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

The reference family and additional controls behave as designed across the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). Owned concept writes rank 1--6 among the 120 compared effects. On the chest sets, the concept write also typically ranks in the top decile: the deficit concerns attribution rather than efficacy. Relabelling the test rows within type shifts the answer far more than the concept write on COCO (label gaps of $-3$ to $-23$ logits in 13 of 19 blocks), but by less than one logit in 14 of 20 NIH blocks. Wording and mapping contrasts are small for most families but large for Mass in Qwen2.5-VL and bottle in Gemma~3 4B, reproducing the seed study's prompt dependence.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Direction and label geometry.} Neither correlation among the six directions nor co-occurrence of the six report labels explains why a competing direction wins (Table~\ref{tab:cf-geometry}). On NIH, the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $0.019$ against $0.023$ for random pairs), and the labels are nearly uncorrelated (mean $\phi=0.026$). Yet NIH shows the same off-diagonal dominance as CheXpert, where directions and labels are correlated (mean cosine $0.24$; Effusion--Consolidation $0.51$, $\phi=0.74$). The own direction wins in 26\% of NIH and 29\% of CheXpert cells. The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction at chance rate (19--23\%) and the most co-occurring label at chance rate (17--25\%). Across the 1{,}140 chest off-diagonal cells, the competitor's advantage $W_{q,d}-W_{q,q}$ is uncorrelated with cosine (Spearman $0.05$, $p=0.11$) and with $\phi$ ($0.003$). Removing each cell's strongest competitor does not restore ownership: 126 of 228 chest cells (55\%) have at least two competitors above the own write. The natural-image families with the highest co-occurrence (person with bicycle, $\phi=0.14$; person with dog; chair with bottle) retain ownership in 98--100\% of cells when the competitor set is restricted to them. In the seed cell, Nodule is the \emph{least} similar of the five directions to Effusion (cosine $-0.06$). It still moves the Effusion answer more than Effusion's own direction ($0.262$ against $0.190$). The most similar direction, Atelectasis (cosine $0.49$), moves it by $0.083$.

\paragraph{Scale, saturation, refits, and numerics.} The grades hold on the logit scale: ownership recomputed from the margin $\ell_{\mathrm{yes}}-\ell_{\mathrm{no}}$ agrees with the probability-scale verdict in 335 of 342 cells, and every owned cell stays owned (Table~\ref{tab:cf-scale}). Saturation runs counter to a ceiling explanation: COCO blocks have far more rows at $P(\mathrm{yes})>0.99$ or $<0.01$ than chest blocks (median block share $0.82$ against $0.18$ and $0.32$). Restricting every cell to its unsaturated rows preserves $O_q>0$ in all 27 owned chest cells and in 32 of the 191 others. The same pattern holds in the clean-No, clean-Yes, label-present, and label-absent strata. Refitting the probes on resampled training patients changes $O_q$ by a median of $0.03$. The bf16 batch effects declared at the gates (candidate-logit differences of $0.07$--$1.84$) leave the verdicts unchanged: the margin sign agrees in 55 of 61 blocks, and the six disagreeing blocks contain no owned cell. Re-scoring the clean baseline inside a second module gives bitwise agreement in 100\% of rows for 22 of 33 blocks and in 75\% for the rest, with $|\Delta P(\mathrm{yes})|>0.1$ in at most 1.9\% of rows. Writes at the connector locus are an order of magnitude smaller than at the primary locus (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $0.009$ against $0.046$ on NIH and $0.008$ against $0.270$ on COCO).

\paragraph{Alternative direction constructions.} The deficit is not specific to the logistic normal. We use the same protocol to write four other directions built from the same projected, train-scaled features (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern $\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta_c$ \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, the normal orthogonalised against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualised against the other five labels. Across the 42 blocks scored so far (14 per dataset), every construction yields ownership in 6--15\% of chest cells (NIH 5, 12, 13, 7, and 6 of 84 for the logistic, difference-of-means, pattern, orthogonalised, and residualised directions; CheXpert 15, 13, 10, 13, and 9 of 84) and 44--86\% of COCO cells (72, 43, 37, 70, and 72 of 84). The difference-of-means and pattern directions are near-collinear with each other and at cosine $0.1$--$0.4$ to the logistic normal. They add a few chest cells and lose many natural-object cells. They are weaker handles everywhere. The chest-to-COCO gap persists. Cells owned under at least one of the five constructions total 19 of 84 on NIH and 28 of 84 on CheXpert against 76 of 84 on COCO. Enlarging the clinical family with every further dataset label that has enough support (three on NIH, six on CheXpert; Table~\ref{tab:cf-extcomp}) creates no ownership. Qwen2.5-VL-7B retains zero owned chest cells with margins of $-0.07$ to $-0.46$.

\paragraph{The direction the reader responds to.} The reader's handle lies in the same representation. For each question, we fit a ridge regression of the model's own clean answer margin on the same features (3{,}000 training rows), lift it like a probe normal, and write it at the same dose against the same reference family (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). This answer direction $a_q$ is a handle where the label direction is not. In Qwen2.5-VL-7B, it is owned for three of six NIH concepts (Effusion $O^a=+0.29$ with an own write of $0.81$, Cardiomegaly, Nodule) and all six CheXpert concepts. In Lingshu 32B, it is owned for three NIH and five CheXpert concepts. In every block, its write beats the label direction's strongest logistic competitor on all six questions. It also differs from the probe normal: their cosine is $0.06$--$0.07$ in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and $0.30$--$0.33$ in Lingshu 32B on the chest sets, against $0.54$--$0.64$ on COCO. On natural images, the readable and writable directions coincide. On chest radiographs, the model answers along a direction the probe does not read.
