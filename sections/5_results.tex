\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Reading is not writing}
\label{sec:headline}

Table~\ref{tab:cf-main} reports scores for every checkpoint on every dataset. Figure~\ref{fig:overview} places every concept cell on the three grades. Both chest datasets show the same pattern. Clinical concepts are readable in 134 of 240 cells (56\%): probe selectivity reaches $0.1$--$0.3$ over matched random-label controls for most findings in most families. The models also answer: 140 of the 228 cells with a scored write matrix (61\%) have a clean-answer AUROC whose lower bound exceeds chance. Of these, 91 cells are both readable and answerable. Yet only 27 cells (12\%) are owned. In 150 cells (66\%), the verdict is \emph{stronger competitor}---a direction fitted for another finding moves the concept's answer more than its own direction does. Readability and answerability are far from sufficient for writing: of the 91 readable and answerable cells, 19 are owned (21\%), against 8 of the remaining 137 (6\%). On the chest sets, ownership is the smallest and least stable of the three grades. Applying an effect floor of $0.05$ to both $W_{q,q}$ and $O_q$ leaves 18 of the 27 owned chest cells (8\% of 228), against 78 of the 100 owned COCO cells (68\%). Refitting the probes on resampled patients leaves 6 of the 10 owned NIH cells with $O_q>0$, against 94 of 100 on COCO. The write itself is not selective on the chest sets. Of the answer change a clinical write produces across the six questions, 24\% lands on its own question on NIH and 22\% on CheXpert, against 79\% on COCO (Figure~\ref{fig:cf-w-structure}). We call this pattern the attribution deficit. The model exposes the concept to a probe, but the probe's direction is not a handle on the concept. Written back, it moves other findings' answers as much as its own.

Figure~\ref{fig:example} shows the pattern on individual images. On a chest radiograph, the Effusion and Nodule writes raise the same set of answers. The competing write raises the Effusion answer more. On a natural image, the dog write moves only the dog answer. The write matrix reveals two parts of the deficit (Figure~\ref{fig:write-matrices}, Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). First, concept writes move their own answers little on the chest sets. The median $W_{q,q}$ is $0.015$ on NIH and $0.024$ on CheXpert against $0.270$ on COCO. Writes meet the steering reference---beating the random family and the sham---in 41 of 228 chest cells against 102 of 114 COCO cells. Second, when a chest write moves the answer, the movement is shared. In 14 of those 41 cells, a competing clinical direction moves the answer as much or more. Across the grid, the Atelectasis, Effusion, and Consolidation directions raise each other's answers by similar amounts. On COCO, the diagonal entry of $W$ dominates its row. On NIH and CheXpert, the row is flat or peaks off the diagonal. In every Qwen2.5-VL size, a competing direction---Cardiomegaly at 3B, Nodule at 7B and 32B, Atelectasis at 72B---raises the Effusion answer more than the Effusion direction does.

\subsection{The seed finding replicates on new patients}
\label{sec:seed}

The evaluation builds on a two-model study of Qwen2.5-VL-7B and LLaVA-1.5-7B on NIH (Appendix~\ref{app:seed}). The central result reproduces on a 600-image cohort disjoint from every earlier cohort (Table~\ref{tab:cf-seed}). The Effusion write raises the Effusion answer by $0.190$, exceeding the random 95th percentile ($0.137$) and sham ($0.017$). This effect ranks second among the 120 compared effects. The Nodule direction raises the Effusion answer by $0.262$. Effusion ownership is $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.071$ (95\% CI $[-0.080,-0.062]$), matching the original $-0.057$ $[-0.069,-0.045]$. All six diagonal contrasts are negative, as in the original ownership matrix. The Mass prompt dependence also reproduces. Effusion and Cardiomegaly are the most readable chest concepts (mean selectivity $0.17$ and $0.19$ over 40 chest blocks). Effusion is owned in 5 of 38 chest blocks, four of them with $O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}\leq0.03$.

\subsection{The write mechanism is intact on natural objects}
\label{sec:coco}

The identical procedure yields the opposite outcome on COCO (Table~\ref{tab:cf-main}, right block). Objects are owned in 100 of 114 cells (88\%), with ownership margins of $0.1$--$0.9$ in the Qwen, Lingshu, and Gemma families. 102 cells meet the steering reference. Only six have a stronger competitor. Seventeen of nineteen checkpoints with a COCO block own at least four of the six objects. The two exceptions, LLaVA-1.5-13B (two) and LLaVA-Med (one), read the objects ($S=0.10$--$0.15$) and answer them. Dose response follows the write. On COCO, median ownership across questions and blocks rises from $-0.03$ at $\alpha=-0.1$ to $+0.26$ at $\alpha=+0.25$. It stays positive at $\alpha=+0.5$ ($+0.18$). On NIH, it stays within $\pm0.1$ at every dose (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}c). The write, direction construction, reference family, and decision rules therefore work as intended. The deficit is specific to clinical concepts.

\subsection{Ownership belongs to the reader}
\label{sec:reader}

Gemma~3 uses one SigLIP tower for its 4B, 12B, and 27B checkpoints, MedGemma uses one MedSigLIP tower for 4B and 27B, and LLaVA-1.5 uses one CLIP tower for 7B and 13B. The primary locus is the tower's final block. Pooled features, fitted probes, and write vectors are therefore identical across sizes within each family (bit-identical for Gemma~3 4B and 12B and for LLaVA-1.5; equal to one bf16 rounding step for Gemma~3 27B and MedGemma 27B, direction cosines above $0.9987$). Readability is the same across sizes by construction. Every difference in Figure~\ref{fig:readers} comes from how the connector and language model read the same written representation. These differences are large relative to every source of noise in the grid (Table~\ref{tab:cf-pairs}). With the identical write on the same 600 patients, Gemma~3 4B and 12B differ in $O_q$ by $-0.83$ $[-0.85,-0.81]$ on NIH Effusion and 12B and 27B by $+0.79$ $[+0.76,+0.82]$. The paired patient-bootstrap interval for $\Delta O_q$ excludes zero for five or six of the six concepts in eleven of the twelve shared-tower pair--dataset combinations. The median $|\Delta O_q|$ is 1.4--3.7 times the families' refit standard deviation (up to 8.8 times for single concepts). Competing directions own the chest answers outright in 12B ($O_{\mathrm{Effusion}}=-0.83$ on both chest sets, $O_{\mathrm{Mass}}=-0.65$, $O_{\mathrm{Pneumothorax}}=-0.71$). In 27B, no direction moves them ($-0.04$, $-0.08$, $-0.01$). In 4B, the clean answer is yes on 94--97\% of chest rows. Every own write lowers its own answer's logit margin more than any competitor raises it ($O^m_q$ from $-1.1$ to $-19.8$, all intervals below zero). MedGemma 4B and 27B differ the same way (Pneumothorax $-0.60$ against $-0.06$ and Edema $+0.10$ against $-0.28$ on CheXpert). LLaVA-1.5 7B and 13B own nothing on either chest set while owning five and two COCO objects with the same write. The owned chest cells of these readers (Gemma~3 12B: Nodule on NIH, Edema on CheXpert; MedGemma 4B: Cardiomegaly and Edema on CheXpert) are the least of this evidence. The reader effect lies in the whole matrix, not in which cell crosses zero.

\subsection{Size and family}
\label{sec:families}

Ownership on the chest sets is concentrated in a few readers and varies by concept and dataset (Figure~\ref{fig:ladders}; Table~\ref{tab:cf-families}). Qwen2.5-VL owns no concepts at 3B and 7B, Cardiomegaly at 32B, and Atelectasis ($O=0.54$), Cardiomegaly, and Mass at 72B; Effusion is anti-owned at every size. Lingshu, the medical Qwen2.5-VL derivative, owns three CheXpert concepts at 7B and five at 32B, the largest clinical ownership in the grid. InternVL3.5 answers every NIH question at 8B, 14B, and 38B, but its writes stay within $0.04$ of zero on the chest sets. It owns five or six COCO objects. LLaVA-1.5 reads the chest concepts, answers none above chance, and owns none. Only Lingshu-32B owns the same concept (Cardiomegaly) on both chest sets.

\subsection{Controls and alternatives}
\label{sec:controls}

The reference family and additional controls behave as designed across the grid (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}d; Table~\ref{tab:cf-controls}). Owned concept writes rank 1--6 among the 120 compared effects. On the chest sets, the concept write also typically ranks in the top decile. The deficit concerns attribution rather than efficacy. Relabelling test rows within type shifts the answer far more than the concept write on COCO (label gaps of $-3$ to $-23$ logits in 13 of 19 blocks). On NIH, relabelling shifts the answer by less than one logit in 14 of 20 blocks. Wording and mapping contrasts are small for most families. Large contrasts for Mass in Qwen2.5-VL and bottle in Gemma~3 4B reproduce the seed study's prompt dependence.

\label{sec:robust-geometry}

\paragraph{Geometry, scale, saturation, refits, numerics.} None of the measurement alternatives explains the deficit (Tables~\ref{tab:cf-geometry} and \ref{tab:cf-scale}; Appendix~\ref{app:cf-robustness}). On NIH, the six directions are as orthogonal as random vectors (mean off-diagonal cosine $0.019$ against $0.023$) and the labels nearly uncorrelated ($\phi=0.026$), yet the own direction wins in 26\% of NIH and 29\% of CheXpert cells. The strongest competitor matches the most similar direction, or the most co-occurring label, at chance rate. Removing it leaves a second competitor above the own write in 55\% of chest cells. The most co-occurring COCO families retain ownership in 98--100\% of cells. The verdicts agree on the logit scale in 335 of 342 cells. COCO blocks are more saturated than chest blocks (median block share $0.82$ against $0.18$ and $0.32$). Restricting every cell to unsaturated rows preserves all 27 owned chest cells. Refits move $O_q$ by a median of $0.03$. The declared bf16 batch effects change no verdict (six disagreeing blocks, no owned cell among them). Connector-locus writes are an order of magnitude smaller than primary-locus writes (median $|W_{q,q}|$ $0.009$ against $0.046$ on NIH, $0.008$ against $0.270$ on COCO).

\paragraph{Alternative direction constructions.} The deficit is not a property of the logistic normal. Four other directions built from the same projected, train-scaled features are written by the same protocol (Table~\ref{tab:cf-altdir}): the difference of class means, the Haufe pattern $\Sigma\boldsymbol\beta_c$ \citep{haufe2014interpretation}, the normal orthogonalised against the other five normals, and the normal refitted on features residualised against the other five labels. Over the 42 blocks scored so far (14 per dataset), the chest sets are owned in 6--15\% of cells under every construction (NIH 5, 12, 13, 7, and 6 of 84; CheXpert 15, 13, 10, 13, and 9 of 84) and COCO in 44--86\% (72, 43, 37, 70, and 72 of 84); the difference-of-means and pattern directions, at cosine $0.1$--$0.4$ to the logistic normal, add a few chest cells and lose many natural-object cells, so they are weaker handles everywhere. Cells owned under at least one construction number 19 of 84 on NIH and 28 of 84 on CheXpert against 76 of 84 on COCO. Enlarging the clinical family with every further dataset label that has enough support (Table~\ref{tab:cf-extcomp}) creates no ownership, and writing the logistic directions with per-token weights, by the softmax of each token's probe score or only on the top quarter of tokens, owns 0 of 6 chest concepts and 6 of 6 COCO objects in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B (Table~\ref{tab:cf-tokenw}): concentrating the write where the probe reads does not create a clinical handle.

\paragraph{The direction the reader responds to.} The handle the reader does have is in the same representation. For each question we fit a ridge regression of the model's own clean answer margin on the same features (3{,}000 training rows), lift it like a probe normal, and write it at the same dose against the same reference family (Table~\ref{tab:cf-ansdir}). This answer direction $a_q$ is a handle where the label direction is not: in Qwen2.5-VL-7B it is owned for three of six NIH concepts (Effusion $O^a=+0.29$ with an own write of $0.81$) and all six CheXpert concepts, in Lingshu 32B for three NIH and five CheXpert concepts, and in every block its write beats the strongest logistic competitor on all six questions. It is also a different direction: its cosine to the probe normal is $0.06$--$0.07$ in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and $0.30$--$0.33$ in Lingshu 32B on the chest sets, against $0.54$--$0.64$ on COCO. On natural images the readable and the writable direction coincide; on chest radiographs the model answers along a direction the probe does not read.
